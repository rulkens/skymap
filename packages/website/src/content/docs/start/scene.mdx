**Measured** is what an instrument recorded: a direction on the sky, a brightness, a photograph. **Derived** is computed from measurements by a stated method: a distance from a redshift, a planet’s position from its orbit. **Drawn** is a model or a choice of ours, with no measurement behind each object: a ring, a glow, a picture of something no camera has seen.

## Earth and its surface

The surface is NASA’s <Source id="nasa-blue-marble">Blue Marble</Source> mosaic of August 2004, with sharper satellite and aerial imagery in some regions, shown as the camera comes closer. The sharpest is Søndermarken, a park in Copenhagen, at 10 centimetres per pixel. The clouds are one fixed composite. The search has 18 places on Earth; see [Finding things](/docs/guide/finding/).

**Measured:** the surface. **Derived:** where Earth is and which side is lit. **Drawn:** the clouds and the atmosphere. The imagery and its licences are under [Imagery](/science/#sources).

## The Moon

The Moon is a sphere at its true size. <Fact id="moon-orbit-light" inline />

**Derived:** its position, less accurately than a planet’s: the Moon follows an average orbit, while the planets are corrected against NASA’s positions. **Drawn:** its surface map, which is Solar System Scope’s, like the planets’.

## Planets and their moons

<Fact id="scene-bodies" inline /> Each is a sphere at its true size, placed where it is at the date and time on the clock. Nine of them have an atmosphere, and only Saturn has rings.

**Derived:** every position, from published orbits. For the eight planets and 18 moons of the giant planets the orbits are corrected against NASA’s <Source id="horizons">JPL Horizons</Source> service for the years 1900 to 2100; Pluto, Charon, the moons of Mars, Puck and Nereid follow average orbits without that correction. **Measured:** the surfaces of Pluto, Charon and the larger moons, which are spacecraft photographs. **Drawn:** the atmospheres, and the maps of seven planets, whose maker says gaps are filled with invented terrain. See [Time](/docs/guide/time/) for setting the date; the orbits’ sources are under [Solar system](/science/#sources).

**Derived:** the positions. **Drawn:** the models. The whale and the petunias are not data.

## Stars

The stars come from <Source id="gaia">Gaia</Source>: the brightest of those brighter than magnitude 14, plus <Source id="gcns">Gaia’s stars within 100 parsecs</Source>, and <Source id="hipparcos">Hipparcos</Source> for the stars too bright for Gaia. Up to 12.9 million load, depending on the data size. The app names 118 of them. Each is drawn where Gaia measured it in 2016, whatever date the clock shows.

<Fact id="scene-constellations" />

**Measured:** a star’s direction, brightness and colour. **Derived:** its distance. **Drawn:** the constellation lines, which are switched on under Labels & Guides in the [Settings](/docs/guide/settings/) panel. The catalogues are under [Stars](/science/#sources).

## The Milky Way

From inside, the Milky Way is the measured stars. From outside it is a generated model, because every camera we have is inside it. At its centre is the black hole Sagittarius A*, with a glowing disc that we invented, and 40 stars that move round it on orbits published after years of tracking them. A blue band along the galaxy’s plane marks the Zone of Avoidance, the part of the sky where its dust hides the galaxies behind it.

**Drawn:** the galaxy from outside, the black hole’s picture and the band. **Derived:** the black hole’s distance and the orbits of the stars around it.

## Nearby galaxies

<Fact id="andromeda-distance" inline /> As the camera approaches, a galaxy is drawn as a point, then a disc, then a picture: a photograph we chose for 81 named galaxies, among them Andromeda and the two Magellanic Clouds, and a survey image for the others.

**Measured:** the direction, and the photograph. **Derived:** the distance, which inside 30 megaparsecs is measured without redshift. **Drawn:** the generated disc shown before a galaxy’s picture loads, and its tilt where no catalogue gives one.

## The galaxy surveys

Every other galaxy is a point from one of four catalogues: <Source id="sdss">SDSS</Source>, <Source id="2mrs">2MRS</Source>, <Source id="glade">GLADE</Source> and <Source id="desi">DESI</Source>. The quasars, from <Source id="milliquas">Milliquas</Source>, are the most distant objects in the scene. <Fact id="survey-gaps" inline />

<Fact id="2mrs-coverage" inline /> DESI appears as three regions cut from its first data release, which are off at first.

**Measured:** direction and redshift. **Derived:** distance, from the redshift and a model of cosmology, and the colour of a point, from two measured brightnesses. How many galaxies load depends on the data size; see [Settings](/docs/guide/settings/). The table under [Galaxies and quasars](/science/#sources) gives the number of objects we draw from each catalogue, and [Known simplifications](/science/#simplifications) lists what the points leave out.

## Clusters, superclusters and voids

Named markers show the large structures. Most come from two catalogues, of <Source id="mcxc">X-ray clusters</Source> and of <Source id="mscc">superclusters</Source>. <Fact id="scene-structure-kinds" inline /> A void is marked with a ring and no glow. <Fact id="laniakea-home" inline />

**Derived:** the positions and sizes of the markers taken from the two catalogues. **Drawn:** the 42 placed by hand, and every disc, ring and name.

## Filaments and the cosmic web

The purple and orange glow is the <Source id="mcpm-vac">density of the cosmic web</Source>, computed by the Monte Carlo Physarum Machine from the positions of SDSS galaxies. Two more layers show the same structure another way: filaments traced along it, and flow ribbons that follow the motion of galaxies apart from the expansion.

**Derived:** all three. Each is the output of an algorithm run on galaxy positions or velocities. The glow is on at first; the other two are switched on in the [Settings](/docs/guide/settings/) panel.

## What is not here

<Fact id="scene-not-here" inline /> Nebulae, star clusters and the International Space Station are not objects in the scene either. A star or a galaxy that the search does not find by name may still be there as an unnamed point; see [Finding things](/docs/guide/finding/).
