Three words are used the same way on every line. **Measured** is what an instrument recorded: a direction on the sky, a brightness, a photograph. **Derived** is computed from measurements by a method someone could repeat: a distance from a redshift, a planet’s position from its orbit. **Drawn** is put there by us so that the picture can be read: a ring, a glow, a model of something no camera has seen. [Science and data](/science/#bins) sorts the whole scene into these three and gives the reasons.

The last column is a first answer. Nearly everything mixes the three, and the sections below take each thing apart. Every statement on this page has its source in the list at the foot.

## Earth and its surface

The surface is satellite photography, with sharper imagery set into some regions as you come down. <Fact id="geodanmark-orthophoto" inline /> <Fact id="sci-earth-clouds" inline /> <Fact id="search-places" inline />

**Measured:** the surface. **Derived:** where Earth is and which side is lit. **Drawn:** the clouds and the glow of the air. The imagery and its licences are under [Imagery](/science/#sources).

## The Moon

<Fact id="moon-orbit-light" />

**Derived:** its position, and less closely than a planet’s. The Moon follows mean orbital elements, an average of its orbit, where the planets are corrected against NASA’s positions. **Drawn:** its surface map, which is Solar System Scope’s (see the next section).

## Planets and their moons

<Fact id="scene-bodies" />

<Fact id="scene-rings" />

Each is a sphere at its true size, where it was or will be at the instant on the clock. Nine of them have an atmosphere.

**Derived:** every position, from published orbits corrected against NASA’s JPL Horizons service for the years 1900 to 2100. **Measured:** the surfaces of Pluto, Charon and the larger moons, which are spacecraft photographs. **Drawn:** the atmospheres, and the maps of seven planets, which their maker says fill gaps with invented terrain. [Time](/docs/guide/time/) says how to set the date, and the orbits’ sources are under [Solar system](/science/#sources).

## Stars

The stars are Gaia’s, brighter than magnitude 14, with every Gaia star within 100 parsecs and Hipparcos for the ones too bright for Gaia. <Fact id="scene-named-stars" inline /> <Fact id="sim-star-epoch" inline />

<Fact id="scene-constellations" />

## The Milky Way

From inside, the Milky Way you see is the measured stars themselves. From outside it is a generated model, because every camera we have is inside it. At its centre is the black hole Sagittarius A*, with a glowing disc that we invented. <Fact id="scene-s-stars" inline /> A blue band along the galaxy’s plane marks the Zone of Avoidance, the part of the sky where its dust hides the galaxies behind.

**Drawn:** the galaxy from outside, the black hole’s picture and the band. **Derived:** the black hole’s distance and the orbits of the stars around it.

## Nearby galaxies

<Fact id="andromeda-distance" />

Close to it a galaxy grows from a point into a disc and then into a picture: a photograph of our choosing for 81 named galaxies, among them Andromeda and the two Magellanic Clouds, and a survey image for the others.

**Measured:** the direction, and the photograph. **Derived:** the distance, which inside 30 megaparsecs comes from methods that do not use redshift. **Drawn:** the generated disc a galaxy wears before its picture arrives, and its tilt where no catalogue gives one.

## The galaxy surveys

Every other galaxy is a point from one of four catalogues: SDSS, 2MRS, GLADE and DESI. The quasars, from Milliquas, are the most distant objects in the scene. <Fact id="survey-gaps" inline /> <Fact id="2mrs-coverage" inline /> <Fact id="scene-desi-off" inline />

**Measured:** direction and redshift. **Derived:** distance, from the redshift and a model of cosmology, and the colour of a point, from two measured brightnesses. How many galaxies load depends on the data size, which [Screens, quality and domes](/docs/guide/screens-and-domes/) explains. Each catalogue, with the number of objects we draw from it, is in the table under [Galaxies and quasars](/science/#sources), and [Known simplifications](/science/#simplifications) lists what the points leave out.

## Clusters, superclusters and voids

Discs and rings with names mark the large structures. Some come from two catalogues, of X-ray clusters and of superclusters. <Fact id="scene-structure-kinds" inline /> A void gets a ring and no glow.

<Fact id="laniakea-home" />

**Derived:** the positions and sizes, from the catalogues or set by hand. **Drawn:** the discs, rings and names.

## Filaments and the cosmic web

<Fact id="cosmic-web-map" />

Two more layers show the same structure another way: lines traced along the filaments, and ribbons that follow the flow of galaxies apart from the expansion. <Fact id="scene-filaments-off" inline /> So are the flows, which have an exhibit of their own.

**Derived:** all three. Each is the output of an algorithm run on galaxy positions or velocities, and none is an image. The glow is on when the app opens; the other two are switched on in [Settings](/docs/guide/settings/).

<Fact id="observable-edge" />

**Drawn:** the sphere. **Derived:** its radius, from a model of cosmology. Nothing at that distance has been observed.

## What is not here

<Fact id="scene-not-here" />

Nebulae, star clusters and the International Space Station are not objects in the scene either. A star or a galaxy that the search does not find by name may still be there as an unnamed point: [Finding things](/docs/guide/finding/) says what the search covers.

The full list of named objects will be in the [Object catalogue](/docs/reference/objects/). For where each catalogue comes from and how it is licensed, see [All sources at a glance](/docs/data/). To fly through the scene in order, start with [First flight](/docs/start/first-flight/).
