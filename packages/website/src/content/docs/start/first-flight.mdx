You need a browser that has WebGPU: [Browser support and troubleshooting](/docs/start/browsers/) lists them. <Fact id="no-account" inline />

The steps give a key where there is one, and a button for a phone or a tablet. Every picture was taken in the app, and the link under it opens the app in that state.

## Open the app

1. Open the app in a second window, so that this page stays beside it.

<p>
  <OpenApp />
</p>

2. Wait for the two buttons on the welcome screen to light up, then press **Explore**. The welcome screen closes and you are above the sunlit side of Earth.

<Fact id="start-welcome" />

The welcome screen is shown once. If you have been here before, the app opens straight on Earth. <Fact id="start-about-button" inline />

<Figure id="start-arrival" />

What is on the screen:

- **At the top**, the search box, then three round buttons: Home, a button that sets the view turning slowly, and About.
- **On the right**, the card of the selected object. Earth is selected when you arrive, which is also why it has a white ring around it. [Info cards](/docs/guide/info-cards/) says what a card holds. On a phone the card is a sheet at the foot of the screen.
- **On the left**, a Navigation panel that lists the main controls, and the [Settings](/docs/guide/settings/) panel. On a phone both start folded.
- **At the lower right**, the scale bar and, under it, the clock.

## Look around

3. Drag anywhere on the sky, with the mouse or with one finger. The stars swing round and Earth stays in the middle.

4. Put the pointer over the sky and scroll, or pinch with two fingers, so that Earth shrinks. Keep going until the scale bar reads a few hundred thousand kilometres. Earth is now a small circle with its name beside it, and the ring around it is the Moon’s orbit, with the Moon on it.

<Figure id="start-moon-orbit" />

<Fact id="moon-orbit-light" />

<Fact id="zoom-inputs" />

Scrolling does nothing while the pointer is over a panel or a card. Move it back over the sky.

<Fact id="scale-bar" />

If you keep pulling back, the orbits of the planets come into view one after another, each with its planet’s name. [Moving around](/docs/guide/moving/) has the rest of the controls, among them the ones that take over close to a surface.

## Go to Saturn by name

5. Press <Keys of="/" />, or click the search box at the top. The search opens over the scene. <Fact id="search-tabs" inline />

6. Type `Saturn`. The cards give way to one result.

<Figure id="start-search" />

7. Press <Keys of="Enter" />, or click the result. The camera flies to Saturn, Saturn’s card takes the place of Earth’s, and the address in the browser now ends in `#focus=body-saturn`.

<Figure id="start-saturn" />

A flight keeps the direction you were looking in, so you may arrive over Saturn’s night side. Drag to come round to the lit one.

<Fact id="search-scope" />

[Finding things](/docs/guide/finding/) covers the search in full. There is a way to go somewhere without it too. <Fact id="double-click-focus" inline />

## Run the clock

<Fact id="start-clock-place" />

8. Pull back from Saturn until the scale bar reads several hundred thousand kilometres. Thin circles appear around the planet and its rings, each with a name on it: these are the orbits of its moons.

9. Press <Keys of="]" /> five times. Without a keyboard, tap the clock and press the button marked › five times. The speed on the clock reads `6 hr/s`, and the moons move along their orbits, the inner ones fastest.

10. Press <Keys of="\" />, or the pause button on the clock. The moons stop, and the address gains the instant the clock stopped on, after `t=`.

<Figure id="start-clock" />

11. Press <Keys of="Shift+N" />, or the **Now** button on the clock. The clock returns to the present and runs again.

<Fact id="start-clock-now" />

<Fact id="clock-keys" />

<Fact id="focus-follows" />

To see a particular date, click the clock’s date and type one. [Time](/docs/guide/time/) explains how, and how far from the present the positions can be trusted.

## Come home

12. Press <Keys of="H" />, or the Home button at the top. The camera flies back to Earth, Earth’s card returns, and the address has nothing after it again.

<Fact id="home-key" />

<Fact id="esc-clears" />

<Note flow>
  With nothing selected the camera no longer travels with a planet. If the clock is running fast, the planet leaves the view. Press <Keys of="H" /> to start again from Earth.
</Note>

## Take the tour

13. Press the About button, the last of the three at the top, then press **Tour** on the welcome screen. The panels go away and the tour’s title comes up.

<Figure id="start-tour" />

14. Let it play, or step through it with the arrow keys or the buttons at the foot of the screen.

<Fact id="grand-tour-steps" />

<Fact id="tour-keys" />

<Fact id="tour-length" />

<Note flow>
  One fault, seen on 7 October 2026: if you step back from the third step to the second, the next step forward ends the tour. Start it again from the search.
</Note>

15. Press <Keys of="Esc" />, or the square button, to leave. The view stays where the tour had taken it, so press <Keys of="H" /> to come back to Earth.

The same tour is in the search, under the Tours tab. [Tours and exhibits](/docs/guide/tours/) lists what else can be played.
