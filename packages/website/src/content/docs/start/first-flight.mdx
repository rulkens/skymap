## Open the app

1. Open the app in a second window, beside this page. In front of a class, keep this page on a second device.

2. Press **Explore** on the welcome screen. If you have been here before, there is no welcome screen. You are above Earth. Earth is selected, so it has a white ring and its card is open: on the right on a computer, as a sheet at the foot of a phone. The search box is at the top, and the scale bar and the clock are at the lower right.

<Figure id="start-arrival" />

## Look around

3. Drag on the sky, with the mouse or one finger. The stars swing round and Earth stays in the middle.

4. With the pointer over the sky, scroll, or pinch two fingers together, until the scale bar reads about 300,000 km. Earth is now a small circle with its name beside it. The line round it is the Moon’s orbit, with the Moon on it, 1.3 seconds away at the speed of light.

<Figure id="start-moon-orbit" />

## Go to Saturn by name

5. Press <Keys of="/" />, or click the search box. The search opens over the scene, with picture cards in seven tabs.

6. Type `Saturn`. The cards give way to one result.

7. Press <Keys of="Enter" />, or click the result. The camera flies to Saturn and Saturn’s card takes the place of Earth’s. If you arrive over the night side, drag round to the lit one. The address now ends in `#focus=body-saturn`: that address is a link to Saturn.

<Figure id="start-search" />

<Figure id="start-saturn" />

## Run the clock

8. Scroll or pinch out until the scale bar reads about 300,000 km again. Thin lines with names appear round the planet: the orbits of its moons.

9. Press <Keys of="]" /> five times. On a phone, tap the clock first: its controls open, with a box for setting a date above them, which you can leave alone. Then tap the Faster button, the arrow to the right of the pause button, five times. The clock reads `6 hr/s`, six hours of the scene for each second, and the moons move along their orbits, the inner ones fastest. The address has gained a date and time, after `t=`.

10. Press <Keys of="\" />, or the pause button on the clock. The moons stop, and `t=` in the address now holds the date and time the clock stopped on.

11. Press <Keys of="Shift+N" />, or the **Now** button on the clock. The clock is back on the present, running at `1 s/s`, and `t=` has gone from the address.

<Figure id="start-clock" />

## Come home

12. Press <Keys of="H" />, or the Home button at the top. The camera flies back to Earth, Earth’s card returns, and the address has nothing after it again.

## Take the tour

13. Press the About button, the last of the three at the top, then **Tour** on the welcome screen. The interface goes away and the tour’s title comes up.

14. Let it play, which takes a little over 5 minutes, or go through its 14 steps with <Keys of="Right" /> and <Keys of="Left" /> or the arrow buttons at the foot of the screen.

15. Press <Keys of="Esc" />, or the square button, to leave. The view stays where the tour took it, so press <Keys of="H" />, or the Home button, to come back to Earth.

<Figure id="start-tour" />
