A picture or a number that comes out of skymap rests on two things: the program that drew it, and the catalogues it drew from. Cite both. Where a survey asks for an acknowledgement as well, this page says so, and [Credits](/docs/credits/) has the text.

## Cite skymap

<Citation full />

<Fact id="sci-cite-skymap" />

The version’s DOI names the release that drew your picture. The other one names the project, whichever release is newest.

## Cite one view

The address is the citation. Every view has one, and it holds what the view shows: the object the camera is on, and the instant on the app’s clock.

<Example to="focus=body-jupiter&t=2020-12-21T18:00:00Z" address label="Open this view">
  Jupiter and Saturn on the evening of 21 December 2020.
</Example>

- `focus=body-jupiter` is the object.
- `t=2020-12-21T18:00:00Z` is the instant, in UTC.

<Fact id="lesson-link" />

Give the address with the day you opened it, as you would for any web page, and name the version of skymap beside it: a later release may draw the same address differently.

<Fact id="share-settings" />

So if a catalogue you switched on matters to your point, say so in words. [Sharing a view](/docs/guide/sharing/) shows how to get an address out of the app, and [URL parameters](/docs/reference/url-parameters/) lists everything one can carry.

## Cite the data, not only the tool

skymap draws other people’s measurements. A distance, a redshift or a position read from it is a catalogue’s figure, and the catalogue’s paper is what to cite for it. These are the papers, source by source.

<CiteRefs />

<Note flow>
  {PAPERS} papers and datasets for {Object.keys(CITE_REFS).length} sources. We opened each at its DOI or its arXiv record on {CHECKED}, and the authors, year, title and journal here are as that record gives them: three authors, then “et al.”.
</Note>

Maps, terrain, 3D models, fonts and code are credited on [Credits](/docs/credits/), with a citation where their makers ask for one.
