## Big screens and projectors

<Fact id="screens-no-fullscreen" />

That is <Keys of="F11" /> on Windows and Linux, and on a Mac <Keys of="Ctrl+Cmd+F" /> or the green button of the window.

<Fact id="hide-interface" />

<Fact id="ref-tab-taken" />

<Fact id="screens-interface-size" />

From the back of a room that is too small to read. The browser’s zoom, <Keys of="Ctrl" /> or <Keys of="Cmd" /> with the plus key, enlarges the panels, the cards and the search.

For a dark room:

1. Open the view you want and make the window fill the screen.
2. Under Display, lower Exposure by one or two EV. A picture set for a lit office is harsh on a wall in the dark.
3. Turn off the names you do not need, or all of them with the switch on Labels & Guides.
4. Press <Keys of="Tab" /> to hide the interface. The clock’s keys and <Keys of="H" /> for Home still work with it hidden. The search is part of the interface: press <Keys of="Tab" /> again before you look for the next object.

[Settings](/docs/guide/settings/#make-it-brighter-or-calmer) describes Exposure, Bloom and the tone curve. None of it is kept over a reload, so set it once the page is open and leave the page open.

## When it stutters

<Fact id="screens-no-quality-setting" />

Try these in order, and stop when the picture moves smoothly.

1. **Choose a smaller data size.** Set Tier, in the title of the Settings panel, to Medium or Small. [Settings](/docs/guide/settings/#see-more-or-fewer-galaxies) has what each holds.
2. **Make the window smaller.** The work for a frame grows with the number of pixels. A window half as wide and half as high has a quarter of them.
3. **Switch layers off.** The glow of the cosmic web, the filaments, the flow and Bloom can each be switched off by their heading, and so can a catalogue of galaxies you are not looking at.
4. **Close what else is using the graphics processor**: other tabs with video or 3D, and other programs.

<Fact id="settings-size-large-choice" />

Dome mode costs more than a flat view of the same window: see [the cost](#what-it-costs) below. If the app does not start at all, or stops with a message, that is for [Browser support and troubleshooting](/docs/start/browsers/#when-the-app-does-not-start).

### Driving it

<Fact id="screens-dome-pointer" />

<Fact id="screens-dome-interface" />

An operator therefore works like this: keep the interface up while finding the next object with <Keys of="/" />, then press <Keys of="Tab" /> to clear it. The clock’s keys, <Keys of="H" /> for Home and <Keys of="Esc" /> all work with the interface hidden. [Controls](/docs/reference/controls/) lists them.

A tour or an exhibit can be opened in dome mode by link, and plays as on a flat screen, with its captions or notes flat over the disc.

### What it costs

<Fact id="screens-dome-cost" />

The disc is as many pixels across as the window’s shorter side, doubled on a screen with two device pixels to one. A full-screen window on a 3,840 by 2,160 display gives a disc 2,160 pixels across. If the picture stutters, the list under [When it stutters](#when-it-stutters) applies, the data size first.

### What a dome’s system has to do

- **Live.** Show the browser window, full screen and with the interface hidden, on the output your system takes a fisheye from. Someone flies the app while the audience watches.
- **As a film.** <Fact id="film-recorder" inline /> <Fact id="film-silent" inline /> The recorder is described under [Command-line tools](/docs/developers/cli/).

The address keeps the dome flag while you fly, so a link copied in dome mode opens in dome mode: [Sharing a view](/docs/guide/sharing/#dome-mode). The flag is spelt in small letters and goes before the `#`; [URL parameters](/docs/reference/url-parameters/) has the forms.
