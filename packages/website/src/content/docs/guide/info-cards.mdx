## Units and distances

<Fact id="card-units" />

<Fact id="card-distance-from-sun" />

A planet, a moon or a spacecraft is the exception: the Distance line at the top of its card is measured from the camera, as described under [Planets and moons](#planets-and-moons).

Other units appear on the cards. M⊕ is the mass of Earth, and g is the pull of gravity at Earth’s surface. M☉, L☉ and R☉ are the mass, the light output and the radius of the Sun. Gyr and Myr are billions and millions of years. Magnitudes run backwards: a smaller number is a brighter object. Each of these is explained by its row’s underlined word.

## What a card gets wrong or leaves out

- **Typed figures do not move with the clock.** Only the Distance line of a body in focus changes. The Voyagers’ distance from the Sun and the rovers’ positions are dated in their text.
- **The planets’ fact sheet names no source.** Check a figure against another source before you quote it; the card’s Wikipedia link is a start.
- **A zero can mean “not known”.** <Fact id="card-galaxy-no-redshift" inline />
- **“fallback” means a stand-in.** A diameter or an orientation marked that way is not a survey’s measurement of that galaxy.
- **Estimates look like measurements.** The temperature, luminosity and radius of a field star are estimates from two numbers, and a galaxy’s type is a guess from its colour unless the galaxy has a name of its own.
- **Counts have a date and a limit.** The Galaxies row of a structure counts the points the app has loaded, and the Moons row of a planet is the count on the day the sheet was typed.
- **No uncertainties.** No figure on any card carries an error bar.

Each simplification the app makes is listed with the others under [Known simplifications](/docs/simplifications/).
