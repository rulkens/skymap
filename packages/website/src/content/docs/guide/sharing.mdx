## The address is the link

<Fact id="share-address" />

Everything after the `#` is written by the app. An address with nothing after it is the home view: Earth, at the present moment.

```
#focus=body-saturn&t=2033-03-14T21:00:00.000Z
```

The parts are joined with `&`, and each is described below. [URL parameters](/docs/reference/url-parameters/) lists every one in a table.

## What the app writes, and when

| Part | What it holds | Written when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `focus` | The object the camera turns about | You fly to an object |
| `t` | An instant, in UTC | You set a date or change the clock’s speed |
| `orientation` | Which way is up | You change Orientation in the Settings panel |
| `exhibit`, `tour` | The exhibit or tour that is open | One opens |
| `pose` | The camera’s position and direction | Never by itself: see [the camera](#the-camera) |

## Copy the link

1. Bring the view to where you want it: the object, the date and, if it matters, the orientation.
2. Pause the clock with <Keys of="\" /> if the moment matters.
3. Select the address in the address bar and copy it. On a phone, use the browser’s own share button.

That link opens the app’s standard view of the object. For a link that also holds the camera, use the “copy URL” button described under [the camera](#the-camera).

## What the person who opens it sees

<Fact id="share-opens" />

<Fact id="link-time-read" />

So the clock is paused, on the date you chose, and the planets, moons and spacecraft are where they were at that instant. Two people who open the same link read the same digits on the clock wherever they are. To let the scene run, press <Keys of="\" />; to return to the present, <Keys of="Shift+N" />.

What differs is the receiver’s own screen.

- **The frame.** A wider or narrower window shows more or less of the scene around the object. A link with a pose keeps the camera’s distance and direction, not the edges of your picture.
- **The data size.** <Fact id="phone-data-size" inline /> A planet, or a named galaxy such as Andromeda, opens at either. <Fact id="share-data-size" inline />
- **The card.** On a phone the card is a sheet at the bottom of the screen: see [Info cards](/docs/guide/info-cards/).

## A link for a lesson

1. Find the object: open the search with <Keys of="/" />, type its name and press <Keys of="Enter" />.
2. Set the date: click the date on the clock at the lower right, type a date and a time in UTC, and press <Keys of="Enter" />. The clock pauses there.
3. Copy the address.
4. Open the link in a new window before the lesson, on the screen you will use, and look at what it shows.

<Fact id="lesson-link" />

The [classroom page](/classroom/) has links made this way, each with a question to ask, and says what the app needs from the room’s computer.

## Worked examples

Each link opens what its line says. The line under it is what follows the app’s address.

```
#focus=body-saturn
```
