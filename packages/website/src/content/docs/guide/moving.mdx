## How the camera moves

<Fact id="focus-meaning" />

The same controls work at every scale, from a few metres above the ground to the whole map.

## Look around an object

Drag, and the scene follows your hand as a globe does: drag to the right and the object turns to the right.

<Figure id="guide-controls" />

The Auto-rotate button in the top bar turns the camera slowly round the focus until you press it again. Which way is up is a setting: see [Settings](/docs/guide/settings/).

## Zoom in and out

One control covers every scale. Keep scrolling out from Earth and you pass the Moon’s orbit, the planets, the nearest stars, the Milky Way and the galaxies around it, without changing mode. The scale bar at the lower right says how large things are at the focus: see [Finding things](/docs/guide/finding/#what-you-are-looking-at).

<Figure id="guide-far" />

## Go somewhere else

Double-click an object, or double-tap it, and the camera flies to it and makes it the focus. For an object that is already selected, press <Keys of="F" /> or the Focus button on its card. For anything that is not on screen, use the search: see [Finding things](/docs/guide/finding/).

While a planet, a moon or a spacecraft is the focus, the camera travels with it as the clock runs. That is why Jupiter stays in the middle of the screen while its moons go round it.

## Slide the view sideways

A drag with the right or the middle button moves the point the camera turns about, without turning. skymap has no free flight, and this is the nearest thing to it: the camera then turns and zooms about a point beside the object. The next double-click or search result puts it back on an object.

## Fly low over a surface

When the camera comes down to less than about half the body’s radius above a planet or a moon, the controls change. A drag that starts on the ground slides the ground under the pointer, and one that starts on the sky looks around. A drag with the right or the middle button tilts the view towards the horizon and turns it. The wheel zooms towards the point under the pointer, and a pinch towards the middle of the screen.

<Figure id="guide-surface" />

The camera cannot pass through the ground: over Earth it stops about 15 metres up. Close to one of the four Mars rovers, or to Søndermarken, the scanned park in Copenhagen, every drag turns the camera round the site.

## Go home

<Keys of="H" />, <Keys of="E" /> and the Home button fly the camera back to the sunlit side of Earth. Earth becomes the selected object and the focus.

<Fact id="esc-clears" />

After <Keys of="Esc" /> the camera still turns about the same point in space, but it no longer travels with the object that was there: if the clock is running fast, a planet leaves the view. Press <Keys of="H" /> to start again from Earth.

## On a touch screen

<Fact id="touch-gestures" />

To reach a surface on a touch screen, double-tap the planet and pinch in. To look at the horizon from there you need a mouse.
