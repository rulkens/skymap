## Show or hide things

Each heading in the panel is one part of the scene, and most carry a switch.

| To show or hide | Use |
| --- | --- |
| Galaxies, one catalogue at a time | The eight switches under Galaxies |
| Stars | The switch on Stars, or the four under it: named stars, Gaia stars, the Sun, the stars round the black hole |
| The glow of the cosmic web | The switch on Cosmic web density |
| The filaments, drawn as lines | The switch on Cosmic web filaments |
| The streams that show how galaxies move | The switch on Flow |
| Rings round clusters, superclusters, voids and groups | The switches under Structures |
| Orbits, the Zone of Avoidance band, the Local Bubble, the constellations | The last four switches under Labels & Guides |

## Make it brighter or calmer

All of this is under Display, the last heading.

<Fact id="settings-exposure" />

For a dark room, lower it by one or two EV. For a projector in a room that is not dark, raise it.

<Fact id="settings-bloom" />

<Fact id="settings-tone-curve" />

<Fact id="sci-tone" />

<Fact id="settings-hdr" />

To change one layer and leave the rest, use the sliders of that layer: Galaxy brightness and Point size under Galaxies and then Advanced, Star brightness and Star size under Stars and then Advanced, and Intensity under Cosmic web filaments and under Flow. The [Reference](/docs/reference/settings/) says what each of the others does.

## See more or fewer galaxies

<Fact id="app-data-size-choice" />

It is marked Tier and offers Small, Medium and Large.

<Fact id="phone-data-size" />

<Fact id="settings-size-large-choice" />

<Fact id="settings-size-totals" />

<Fact id="sim-data-sizes" />

<Figure id="guide-size-small" />

<Figure id="guide-size-large" />

The link under each picture opens the same place at the size your screen starts with; set Tier to see the others. The second picture is brighter as well as fuller: the light of every galaxy is added to the picture, so more galaxies in one place make a brighter place. Lower Galaxy brightness, or Exposure, if the middle runs to white.

<Fact id="settings-size-cost" />

A larger size also takes more of the graphics processor’s memory and more work for each frame. If the app stutters after a change, go back down: [Screens, quality and domes](/docs/guide/screens-and-domes/#when-it-stutters) has the other things to try.

<Fact id="ref-settings-tier-reload" />

<Fact id="settings-size-address" />

The table of what each size holds, catalogue by catalogue, is in the [Reference](/docs/reference/settings/#data-size), and the counts of the catalogues themselves are on the [Science page](/science/#sources).

## Even out the surveys

No survey looked at the whole sky to the same depth, and the map shows it. One setting takes some of that unevenness out of the picture, and it is on when the app opens.

<Fact id="settings-density-how" />

<Fact id="settings-density-why" />

<Figure id="guide-density-corrected" />

<Figure id="guide-density-raw" />

The link under each picture opens the app as it starts, with the correction on. To see the second picture yourself, open Galaxies, then Advanced, and set Density correction to “None — raw catalogue”.

<Fact id="sim-reweight" />

<Fact id="settings-density-limits" />

So neither picture is the true one. With the correction, a real crowd of galaxies is toned down along with a crowd that only the catalogue made. Without it, you see each catalogue as it is, its habits included. For anything you mean to measure or to show as evidence, choose None and say so.

<Fact id="settings-density-others" />

The two fans of SDSS, the empty band behind the Milky Way and the thinning of the map with distance are not touched by any of this: [where the map is blind](/science/#simplifications) is on the Science page.
