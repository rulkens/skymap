## Read the clock

<Fact id="clock-readout" />

UTC is the time on the Greenwich meridian, without summer time. The clock never shows your local time, so two people who open the same link read the same digits wherever they are.

## The years the positions hold for

<Fact id="ephemeris-span" />

<Fact id="sim-ephemeris-span" />

The clock does not stop or warn you at either end. A date in 1610 or 3000 gives a scene that looks as sure of itself as today’s, and is not. How the positions are computed is on the [Science page](/science/).

## A date in a link

<Fact id="link-time-write" />

Pause the clock before you copy the address, and the link opens on the moment you were looking at.

<Fact id="link-time-read" />

To write a link by hand, put the date after the `#`, and join it to anything else there with `&`:

```
#focus=body-jupiter&t=2033-03-14T21:00:00Z
```

[Sharing a view](/docs/guide/sharing/) covers the rest of the address, and [URL parameters](/docs/reference/url-parameters/) lists every part of it.

## Things to watch

Each link below opens with the clock paused on a date. Press <Keys of="]" /> until the clock reads the speed named, then <Keys of="\" /> to let it run.

### Io going round Jupiter

<Fact id="io-lap" />

<Figure id="class-time" />

Set the speed to 6 hr/s. The four moons keep their order outwards, and each one out is slower than the one inside it.

### A day on Earth

<Fact id="earth-rotation" />

At 1 hr/s that is one turn in about 24 seconds. Watch the line between day and night stay where it is while the continents pass under it, and the city lights come on behind it.

### The two solstices

<Fact id="solstices-2026" />

<Figure id="lesson-earth-june" />

<Figure id="lesson-earth-december" />

Both pictures are taken from the Sun’s side at 12:00 UTC. In June the Arctic is turned towards the Sun and in December Antarctica is. To pass from one to the other, open the first and run the clock at 1 wk/s: six months go by in under half a minute.

### Jupiter and Saturn in line

<Fact id="great-conjunction-2020" />

<Fact id="conjunction-in-app" />

<Figure id="guide-conjunction" />

The Solar System exhibit shows it from above. Run the clock at 1 mo/s and Jupiter, on the inner orbit, draws ahead of Saturn. Exhibits are described under [Tours and exhibits](/docs/guide/tours/).

## The keys

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| <Keys of="]" /> | One speed faster |
| <Keys of="[" /> | One speed slower |
| <Keys of="\" /> | Pauses the clock, or lets it run |
| <Keys of="Shift+N" /> | Back to the present, at real speed |

Every key the app has is listed under [Controls](/docs/reference/controls/).
