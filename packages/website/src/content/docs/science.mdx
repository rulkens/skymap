### Distance from redshift

<Fact id="sci-galaxy-distance" />

<Formula
  say="d equals c over H nought, times the integral from 0 to z of d z over E of z, where E of z is the square root of omega m times one plus z cubed, plus omega lambda."
  of={['d = \\frac{c}{H_0} ∫_0^z \\frac{d z′}{E(z′)}', 'E(z) = \\sqrt{Ω_m (1 + z)^3 + Ω_Λ}']}
/>

The formula is the line-of-sight [comoving distance](/docs/reference/glossary/#comoving-distance) of [Hogg’s “Distance measures in cosmology”](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9905116); [Lambda-CDM](/docs/reference/glossary/#lambda-cdm) is in the glossary.

### Brightness

A galaxy’s absolute magnitude M follows from a catalogued magnitude m and its distance d in megaparsecs.

<Formula say="Absolute magnitude M equals apparent magnitude m, minus 5 times the logarithm of the distance in megaparsecs, minus 25." of={'M = m − 5 log_{10}\\, d − 25'} />

The 25 is the step from megaparsecs to the 10 parsecs at which [absolute magnitude](/docs/reference/glossary/#absolute-magnitude) is defined.

<Fact id="method-galaxy-light" />

<Formula
  say="Light equals 10 to the power of minus 0.4 times M minus the median M, divided by the square of D over 30."
  of={'\\text{light} = \\frac{10^{−0.4 (M − M_{med})}}{(D / 30)^2}'}
/>

On the way to the screen that light is changed again, by distance and by the density correction, and it cannot be compared between two catalogues: see [Brightness, exposure and colour](/docs/simplifications/#brightness-exposure-and-colour).

### Size, tilt and shape

<Fact id="method-galaxy-size" />

<Formula say="The logarithm of R equals minus 0.249 times M B plus 21, plus 1.366." of={'log\\, R = −0.249 (M_B + 21) + 1.366'} />

A galaxy with neither a size nor an estimate is given a default, and the distance in that sum is not the one that belongs there: see [Sizes](/docs/simplifications/#sizes).

The measured tilts are SDSS’s own, the <Source id="2mass-xsc">2MASS Extended Source Catalog</Source>’s for 2MRS and <Source id="hyperleda">HyperLEDA</Source>’s for GLADE; a galaxy with none is given one computed from its identifier (same section). [Techniques](/docs/rendering/techniques/#galaxies) says how a galaxy goes from a point to a disc to a picture.

### Position and distance

A star’s position, its brightness in the G band and its colour, BP minus RP, are <Source id="gaia">Gaia</Source>’s; [the pipeline page](/docs/data/pipeline/#stars) says which stars are loaded.

<Fact id="sci-star-distance" />

<Source id="bailer-jones">Bailer-Jones and others (2021)</Source> explain why one over the [parallax](/docs/reference/glossary/#parallax) fails for most stars: the error of a small parallax is a large and lopsided error in distance.

### Brightness and colour

<Fact id="method-star-magnitude" />

<Fact id="method-star-light" />

<Formula
  say="M equals m minus 5 times the logarithm of d over 10 parsecs. Light equals 10 to the power of minus 0.4 M, divided by the square of r over 10 parsecs."
  of={['M = m − 5 log_{10}(\\frac{d}{10 \\,\\text{pc}})', '\\text{light} = \\frac{10^{−0.4 M}}{(r / 10 \\,\\text{pc})^2}']}
/>

<Fact id="method-star-tint" />

The five colours are chosen to look right: see [Brightness, exposure and colour](/docs/simplifications/#brightness-exposure-and-colour). The named stars and the stars around the black hole are coloured otherwise: from the temperature in our list, along the curve a glowing body follows, in [Tanner Helland’s approximation](https://tannerhelland.com/2012/09/18/convert-temperature-rgb-algorithm-code.html). Its author made it for editing photographs and says it is not accurate enough for science.

### What a star’s card adds

<Fact id="method-star-card" />

<Formula
  say="5040 over T equals b nought plus b one C plus b two C squared. R equals the square root of L, times the square of 5772 over T."
  of={['\\frac{5040}{T} = b_0 + b_1 C + b_2 C^2', 'R = \\sqrt{L} × (\\frac{5772}{T})^2']}
/>

[Andrae and others 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.09374) put the error of their own luminosities at about 15 per cent and of their radii at 10; ours use the colour alone and are rougher. The temperature relation is that of [Mucciarelli, Bellazzini and Massari 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03882). The 118 named stars do not go through any of this: their figures are typed in from <Source id="seed-famous-stars">a list compiled by hand</Source>.

### Positions

<Fact id="sci-ephemeris" />

An orbit from <Source id="jpl-elements">JPL’s elements</Source> is one ellipse whose shape drifts at a steady rate; the real planets pull on one another, and the fit to <Source id="horizons">JPL Horizons</Source> takes that up.

<Fact id="method-kepler-error" />

What happens outside 1900 to 2100 is under [The Solar System](/docs/simplifications/#the-solar-system).

<Fact id="sci-planet-maps" />

### Atmospheres

The atmospheres of 9 bodies are computed, not photographed. [Bruneton and Neyret (2008)](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8659.2008.01245.x) compute once how much light the air passes and scatters and look the answer up for every pixel; [Hillaire (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1111/cgf.14050) adds a cheaper treatment of light scattered many times. We wrote our own code for both, and each body has a table of gases and hazes.

### Density, filaments and flows

<Fact id="sci-density-field" />

The catalogue is described by [Wilde and others (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02719); it has <Source id="mcpm-vac">a page here</Source>. It is built from redshift distances, so it inherits their distortions.

<Fact id="sci-filaments" />

The threshold of 5 standard deviations and two passes of smoothing are our settings, not the recommendation of DisPerSE’s paper, [Sousbie (2011)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1009.4015).

<Fact id="sci-flows" />

The reconstruction is published with [Courtois and others (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01308). We draw it as it comes.

## What is only drawn

<Fact id="sci-zoa-band" />

Names, orbit lines, constellation lines, the selection ring and the scale bar are drawn too, and none of them stands for a measurement of its own. The glow around bright objects and the tone curve act on the whole frame: see [Bloom, tone curves and HDR](/docs/rendering/techniques/#bloom-tone-curves-and-hdr).
