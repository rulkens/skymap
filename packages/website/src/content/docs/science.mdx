[The science behind skymap](/science/) is the one-screen summary and uses three words. Here the third is split in two, [modelled](/docs/reference/glossary/#modelled) and drawn, so that a model with published numbers is not confused with a choice of ours.

A point on screen is usually several of these at once. Where a rule departs from the sky, the section ends with a link to [Known simplifications](/docs/simplifications/), which lists every departure with its size.

Everything shares one frame: <Fact id="sci-directions" inline /> Positions are kept in megaparsecs from the Sun.

### Distance from redshift

<Fact id="sci-galaxy-distance" />

<Formula say="d equals c over H nought, times the integral from 0 to z of d z over E of z, where E of z is the square root of omega m times one plus z cubed, plus omega lambda.">
  d = (c / H₀) × ∫₀ᶻ dz′ / E(z′), with E(z) = √(Ωm (1 + z)³ + ΩΛ)
</Formula>

The formula is the line-of-sight [comoving distance](/docs/reference/glossary/#comoving-distance) of [Hogg’s “Distance measures in cosmology”](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9905116); [Lambda-CDM](/docs/reference/glossary/#lambda-cdm) is in the glossary.

### Brightness

<Fact id="sci-magnitudes" />

<Formula say="Absolute magnitude M equals apparent magnitude m, minus 5 times the logarithm of the distance in megaparsecs, minus 25.">
  M = m − 5 log₁₀(d) − 25, with d in megaparsecs
</Formula>

[Absolute magnitude](/docs/reference/glossary/#absolute-magnitude) is the glossary’s; the 25 is the step from megaparsecs to the 10 parsecs it is defined at.

<Fact id="method-galaxy-light" />

<Fact id="depart-relative-brightness" />

On the way to the screen that light is changed again, by distance and by the density correction: see [Brightness, exposure and colour](/docs/simplifications/#brightness-exposure-and-colour).

### Position and distance

<Fact id="sci-star-observables" />

<Fact id="sci-star-distance" />

The estimates are those of <Source id="bailer-jones">Bailer-Jones and others (2021)</Source>, who explain why one over the [parallax](/docs/reference/glossary/#parallax) fails for most stars: the error of a small parallax is a large and lopsided error in distance.

### Brightness and colour

<Fact id="method-star-magnitude" />

<Fact id="method-star-light" />

<Fact id="method-star-tint" />

<Fact id="depart-star-tint" />

The named stars and the stars around the black hole are coloured otherwise: from the temperature in our list, along the curve a glowing body follows, in [Tanner Helland’s approximation](https://tannerhelland.com/2012/09/18/convert-temperature-rgb-algorithm-code.html). Its author made it for editing photographs and says it is not accurate enough for science.

### What a star’s card adds

<Fact id="method-star-card" />

The two papers are [Mucciarelli, Bellazzini and Massari 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03882) and [Andrae and others 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.09374); the second puts the error of its own luminosities at about 15 per cent and of its radii at 10. Ours use the colour alone and are rougher. The 118 named stars do not go through any of this: their figures are typed in from <Source id="seed-famous-stars">a list compiled by hand</Source>.

### Positions

<Fact id="sci-ephemeris" />

The elements are <Source id="jpl-elements">JPL’s</Source> and the positions fitted against are <Source id="horizons">JPL Horizons</Source>’. An orbit from elements is one ellipse whose shape drifts at a steady rate; the real planets pull on one another.

### Atmospheres

<Fact id="sci-atmospheres" />

<Fact id="method-atmosphere-earth" />

The method is that of [Bruneton and Neyret (2008)](https://github.com/ebruneton/precomputed_atmospheric_scattering), who compute once how much light the air passes and scatters and look the answer up for every pixel, with the cheaper treatment of light scattered many times from [Hillaire (2020)](https://sebh.github.io/publications/). We wrote our own code for both.

## The Milky Way

Modelled.

<Fact id="sci-milky-way" />

<Figure id="milky-way-drawn" />

<Fact id="method-galactic-centre" />

<Fact id="method-disc-radius" />

<Fact id="sci-milky-way-points" />

The references are [Freudenreich 1998](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9707340), a model of the disc and bar fitted to the COBE satellite’s infrared maps, and [Wegg and Gerhard 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1308.0593), who measured the bar’s angle as 27 ± 2 degrees. Freudenreich’s own bar stands at 14 degrees; we took the later figure. The Gaia stars near the Sun are a separate, measured layer that the model does not replace.

### Density, filaments and flows

<Fact id="sci-density-field" />

The catalogue is described by [Wilde and others (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.02719); its page here is <Source id="mcpm-vac" />. It is built from redshift distances, so it inherits their distortions.

<Fact id="sci-filaments" />

DisPerSE is [Sousbie’s (2011)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1009.4015). The threshold of 5 standard deviations and two passes of smoothing are our settings, not the paper’s recommendation.

<Fact id="sci-flows" />

The reconstruction is published with [Courtois and others (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01308). We draw it as it comes.
