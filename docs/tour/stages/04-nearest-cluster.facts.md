## Candidate 1
- **Fact:** A new class of object drifts alone in the Virgo Cluster: small clumps of young blue stars ("blue blobs"), hundreds of thousands of light-years from any galaxy, formed from gas ripped out of a galaxy as it plunged into the cluster's hot gas — like a belly-flop forcing water out behind you. They dissolve within ~500 million years.
- **Why surprising / lesser-known:** Whole little star clusters being born in the *empty space between* galaxies, with no parent galaxy nearby.
- **Source:** U. Arizona (Jones et al.), 2022 (arXiv:2205.01695). https://news.arizona.edu/news/mysterious-blue-blobs-reveal-new-kind-star-system
- **Recency:** 2022
- **Confidence:** high — press release + arXiv paper retrieved.
- **On screen:** Not shown — sub-galaxy star clusters, invisible at cluster scale.
- **New feature:** render intracluster "blue blobs" / stripped-gas knots between galaxies (needs an intracluster-medium layer). The "belly-flop" framing is vivid but needs the visual.

## Candidate 2
- **Fact:** A "dark" hydrogen cloud in Virgo ~600,000 light-years long, thought star-free since 2007, turns out to host a faint patch of newborn blue stars — one of the most extreme gas-to-stars ratios known (thousands to one).
- **Why surprising / lesser-known:** A cloud assumed empty for ~17 years was quietly forming stars, alone in the space between galaxies.
- **Source:** Jones et al., *ApJL*, 2024. https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.3847/2041-8213/ad3ef5
- **Recency:** 2024
- **Confidence:** high — published article retrieved.
- **On screen:** Not shown — intergalactic HI gas, not rendered.
- **New feature:** render HI gas clouds in the cluster (an intracluster-gas overlay). Same feature family as Candidate 1.

## Candidate 3
- **Fact:** New Event Horizon Telescope images of M87's supermassive black hole — at the heart of Virgo's biggest galaxy — show the dark "shadow" ring stayed exactly the same size from 2017–2021 (~44 microarcseconds, matching Einstein), but the magnetic field swirling around it flipped direction.
- **Why surprising / lesser-known:** The famous 2019 image is static in memory; the magnetism around it is dynamic and reversed, even as the ring stayed rock-steady.
- **Source:** EHT Collaboration, *A&A* 2025. https://phys.org/news/2025-09-images-reveal-unexpected-polarization-flips.html
- **Recency:** 2025
- **Confidence:** high — phys.org write-up of the EHT result retrieved.
- **On screen:** Not shown — M87 is a single point at cluster scale; the black hole and its ring are far below resolution.
- **New feature:** a "zoom to M87" beat that swaps in the EHT ring image (a special-case high-res inset, like a famous-galaxy thumbnail but for the black hole). Self-contained but bespoke.

## Candidate 4 (backup)
- **Fact:** By reading which way "jellyfish" galaxies' stripped gas tails point, astronomers estimate a galaxy's bright stripping tail stays visible only ~1.7 billion years — a brief, dramatic phase as it falls into a cluster and has its gas blown away.
- **Why surprising / lesser-known:** A short clock on a violent transformation, read from tail direction like a windsock.
- **Source:** Salinas, Jaffé, Smith et al., *MNRAS* 533, 2024 (arXiv:2408.03396). https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/533/1/341/7722025
- **Recency:** 2024
- **Confidence:** medium — figures from the search excerpt; cluster-generic, not Virgo-specific.
- **On screen:** Not shown — sub-galaxy gas tails.
- **New feature:** render "jellyfish" galaxies with stripping tails on cluster infall (per-galaxy tail sprites for infalling members). Visually striking if built.
