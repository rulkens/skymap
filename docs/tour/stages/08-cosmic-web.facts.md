## Candidate 1
- **Fact:** In 2021 astronomers found the first evidence that cosmic filaments — strands hundreds of millions of light-years long but only a few million across — actually spin, making them the largest known objects with rotation. Galaxies inside drift along corkscrew paths.
- **Why surprising / lesser-known:** No known mechanism should torque a structure this enormous, so the result implies an unknown process.
- **Source:** Wang, Libeskind et al., *Nature Astronomy* / arXiv:2106.05989, 2021. https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.05989
- **Recency:** 2021
- **Confidence:** high — arXiv abstract retrieved.
- **On screen:** Inferable — filaments ARE shown; their *spin* is not animated. The subject is visible, the motion isn't.
- **New feature:** animate galaxies along helical/rotational paths within filaments — a natural extension of the flow-field layer (stage 06). Makes "the largest spinning things" visible.

## Candidate 2 — WELL-GROUNDED
- **Fact:** In 2025, astronomers used fast radio bursts — millisecond flashes from distant galaxies — as cosmic flashlights to locate the universe's long-missing ordinary matter, finding ~76% of it hides as invisibly thin gas strung between galaxies in the cosmic web.
- **Why surprising / lesser-known:** About half the universe's normal matter was unaccounted for; the trick measures how each burst is delayed by the faint intergalactic fog, weighing gas too dim to see.
- **Source:** Connor, Ravi et al., *Nature Astronomy*, 16 Jun 2025 (arXiv:2409.16952). https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.16952
- **Recency:** 2025
- **Confidence:** high — f_IGM = 0.76 confirmed in the arXiv abstract.
- **On screen:** Inferable — the **MCPM density volume IS that diffuse intergalactic gas/web**. This fact and the on-screen visual are the same thing; the volume literally shows where the missing baryons live. Strongest fit at this stage.
- **New feature:** none. (Optionally: a one-off FRB "flashlight" animation, but the volume already grounds it.)

## Candidate 3
- **Fact:** In 2024 the "Big Ring" was announced — a ring of galaxies ~1.3 billion light-years across, sitting 9.2 billion away, larger than the ~1.2-billion-ly theoretical limit on structures. It's the same discoverer's second giant structure, 12° from the "Giant Arc."
- **Why surprising / lesser-known:** Two record-breaking structures, cosmic neighbours on the sky, doubling the puzzle for the cosmological principle.
- **Source:** Lopez, Clowes & Williger, *JCAP* 2024(7):055. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Ring
- **Recency:** 2024
- **Confidence:** high (claim) — figures confirmed; "challenges the cosmological principle" framing is contested.
- **On screen:** Not shown — at 9.2 Gly it's far beyond this 90-Mpc framing and not in the catalog. Also appears in the stage-00 pool; use at most once.
- **New feature:** a featured distant large-scale-structure overlay (the Big Ring + Giant Arc as marker rings/arcs at their real distance). Notable build.

## Candidate 4
- **Fact:** In 2025 the eROSITA X-ray telescope imaged a single thread of the cosmic web ~23 million light-years long connecting four galaxy clusters — gas at ~10 million degrees weighing ~10 trillion Suns, a direct look at where some "missing" matter lives.
- **Why surprising / lesser-known:** This warm-hot filament gas is normally far too faint to detect; capturing one intact strand, not a statistical average, is rare.
- **Source:** Migkas et al. (Leiden), *A&A*, Jun 2025; via Earth.com. https://www.earth.com/news/scientists-think-they-found-some-of-the-missing-matter-in-the-universe-cosmic-filaments/
- **Recency:** 2025
- **Confidence:** medium — figures from the popular-science write-up; primary A&A not fetched.
- **On screen:** Inferable — filaments + the MCPM volume already show inter-cluster gas threads, so the subject is on screen. Pairs naturally with Candidate 2.
- **New feature (optional):** colour the filament/volume by gas temperature so the "10-million-degree warm-hot gas" reads visually.
