## Candidate 1 — RELEVANT AT THIS FRAMING
- **Fact:** The famous prediction that the Milky Way and Andromeda will smash together in ~4.5 billion years is no longer a sure thing. A 2025 study running 100,000 simulations puts the odds of a merger within 10 billion years at roughly a coin flip (~50%), with only ~2% chance of a head-on hit in the next 4–5 billion years.
- **Why surprising / lesser-known:** Textbooks still present the collision as inevitable; the Large Magellanic Cloud's perpendicular pull can nudge the two past each other.
- **Source:** "Apocalypse When? Hubble Casts Doubt…" — NASA Science (Sawala et al., *Nature Astronomy*), 2025. https://science.nasa.gov/missions/hubble/apocalypse-when-hubble-casts-doubt-on-certainty-of-galactic-collision/
- **Recency:** 2025
- **Confidence:** high — NASA release on a peer-reviewed *Nature Astronomy* paper (arXiv:2408.00064); fetched and confirmed.
- **On screen:** Inferable — both galaxies are in frame, so "these two will (maybe) collide" is grounded by what's visible. The natural home for this fact (it's literally the Andromeda stage).
- **New feature (optional):** draw the predicted motion vectors / the cone of possible future paths between MW and M31.

## Candidate 2
- **Fact:** Andromeda's small companion galaxies are bizarrely lopsided: all but one of its 37 known dwarf satellites sit within 107° of the direction toward us. Such an extreme one-sided arrangement appears in fewer than ~0.3% of simulated galaxies.
- **Why surprising / lesser-known:** We imagine satellites scattered evenly; Andromeda's huddle on the side facing us, a genuine puzzle for the standard model.
- **Source:** Leibniz Institute (AIP) on Kanehisa et al., *Nature Astronomy*, 2025 (arXiv:2504.08047). https://www.aip.de/en/news/andromedas-asymmetrical-challenge-cosmology/
- **Recency:** 2025
- **Confidence:** high — institutional release on a peer-reviewed paper.
- **On screen:** Not shown — M31's dwarf satellites aren't rendered as a distinguished set at this framing.
- **New feature:** highlight M31's satellite galaxies as a labelled set (and the Milky-Way direction) so the one-sidedness is visible. Moderate (needs a satellite-membership layer for M31).

## Candidate 3
- **Fact:** Andromeda's near-invisible gas halo is so vast it already overlaps our own. Mapped by Hubble, it reaches ~1.3 million light-years toward us — roughly halfway to the Milky Way. If we could see it, it would span about three times the width of the Big Dipper.
- **Why surprising / lesser-known:** We picture Andromeda as a tidy spiral 2.5 Mly away; its gaseous envelope means our two galaxies are, in a sense, already touching.
- **Source:** "Hubble Maps Giant Halo Around Andromeda" — NASA Science (Project AMIGA; Lehner et al., ApJ), 2020. https://science.nasa.gov/missions/hubble/hubble-maps-giant-halo-around-andromeda-galaxy/
- **Recency:** 2020
- **Confidence:** high — NASA release on a peer-reviewed ApJ paper.
- **On screen:** Not shown — the halo is invisible gas; nothing renders it.
- **New feature:** render MW + M31 gas halos as translucent spheres so the overlap is visible. This is a strong visual ("the galaxies already touch") and a self-contained feature (two translucent shells at known radii).
