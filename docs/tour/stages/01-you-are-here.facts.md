## Candidate 1 — RELEVANT AT THIS FRAMING
- **Fact:** The Milky Way's disk isn't flat — its outer edge is bent like a warped vinyl record, and that warp slowly wobbles around the galaxy like a spinning top, completing one turn every 600–700 million years. The wobble is so fast that a past collision with a smaller galaxy most likely drives it.
- **Why surprising / lesser-known:** People imagine our galaxy as a tidy flat spiral; in reality it's twisted, the twist is precessing, and that speed is itself evidence of an ancient collision.
- **Source:** ESA Gaia — "Milky Way's warp caused by galactic collision, Gaia suggests," Poggio et al. https://www.esa.int/Science_Exploration/Space_Science/Gaia/Milky_Way_s_warp_caused_by_galactic_collision_Gaia_suggests (2025 follow-up: Poggio et al., *A&A* 699, A199)
- **Recency:** 2020 (warp precession); 2025 "great wave" follow-up
- **Confidence:** medium — precession rate from ESA/phys.org coverage; the detection is debated, so present as a leading finding, not settled.
- **On screen:** Inferable — the *shape of the disk* is exactly what the impostor shows. This is the one fact at this framing whose subject is visible.
- **New feature (small):** give the MW impostor a subtle warp at its outer rim (and optionally a slow precession), so the fact is directly visible rather than merely asserted. Without it the disk reads flat and the fact is told, not shown.
