## Candidate 1 — WELL-GROUNDED
- **Fact:** The Boötes Void is so empty that if our Milky Way sat at its centre, we might not have discovered other galaxies exist until the 1960s — decades after we actually did (the 1920s). It's ~330 million light-years across, yet only ~60 galaxies have been found inside it where a uniform universe would hold ~2,000.
- **Why surprising / lesser-known:** The "60 vs 2,000" deficit and the thought experiment make abstract emptiness vivid: a civilization inside could have thought its galaxy was the whole universe.
- **Source:** "What Is The Boötes Void?" — IFLScience (quotes Greg Aldering; ~330 Mly; 60-vs-2,000). https://www.iflscience.com/what-is-the-bootes-void-the-mysterious-hole-in-the-universe-71083
- **Recency:** discovery 1981; figures long-established.
- **Confidence:** high — multiple independent sources agree on the quote and counts.
- **On screen:** Shown — the void IS the visual, and its sparse galaxy count vs the surrounding web is directly visible. The strongest possible fit; what's on screen *is* the fact.
- **New feature:** none. (Optionally show a galaxy count, but the visible sparsity already carries it.)

## Candidate 2
- **Fact:** Our entire neighborhood may sit inside a giant under-dense bubble — the KBC void or "Local Hole" — and galaxies flowing outward from it could artificially inflate our local measurement of the universe's expansion rate. A 2025 study found the data favor a smaller void than first proposed — enough to soften, but probably not fully resolve, the famous "Hubble tension."
- **Why surprising / lesser-known:** It ties an everyday-sounding idea ("we live in a slightly emptier patch") to one of cosmology's biggest open problems — and the 2025 verdict is an honest "it's complicated."
- **Source:** Stiskalek, Desmond & Banik, 2025 (arXiv:2506.10518). https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10518
- **Recency:** 2025
- **Confidence:** medium — title/authors/method confirmed; PDF body didn't fetch cleanly, so keep wording general (no exact H0 values).
- **On screen:** Not shown — the Local Hole is a subtle, large-scale density deficit *around us*, not the specific void on screen. We're inside it, so there's nothing to point at.
- **New feature:** render the KBC/Local under-density as a wide, faint low-density bubble centred on the Local Group (a big translucent shell in the density field). Conceptual; ties to the Hubble-tension story.
