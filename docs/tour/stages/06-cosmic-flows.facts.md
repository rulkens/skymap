## Candidate 1 — WELL-GROUNDED
- **Fact:** In 2024, Cosmicflows-4 data gave a 60% chance that Laniakea — the half-billion-light-year supercluster we call home — is itself just a suburb of a far bigger gravitational "basin" centred on the distant Shapley Concentration, potentially ~10× larger in volume.
- **Why surprising / lesser-known:** The 2014 "Laniakea" result was our famous cosmic address; this suggests that address may be too small — our surveys still can't see the full structure we belong to.
- **Source:** Valade et al., *Nature Astronomy*, 2024 (arXiv:2409.17261); figures via U. Hawaiʻi News. https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.17261
- **Recency:** 2024
- **Confidence:** high — arXiv abstract + UH release retrieved and confirmed.
- **On screen:** Inferable — the flow field shows streamlines converging into basins; "Laniakea is part of something bigger" is grounded if the convergence toward Shapley is in view.
- **New feature (optional):** render basin-of-attraction watershed boundaries (the surface separating which way matter flows) so "our basin vs the bigger one" is explicit.

## Candidate 2
- **Fact:** Our galaxy isn't only pulled — it's pushed. In 2017 astronomers identified the "Dipole Repeller," a vast, nearly empty region on the opposite side of the sky that shoves the Local Group away with about as much force as the Great Attractor pulls.
- **Why surprising / lesser-known:** Gravity "only attracts," so a region of *nothing* pushing galaxies is deeply counter-intuitive — and it went undetected for decades because empty space emits almost no light.
- **Source:** Hoffman, Pomarède, Tully & Courtois, *Nature Astronomy*, 2017 (arXiv:1702.02483). https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.02483
- **Recency:** 2017 (basis of the modern push-pull picture)
- **Confidence:** high — arXiv abstract retrieved.
- **On screen:** Inferable — *if* the flow field shows streamlines diverging away from an under-dense region. The push is visible only where there's nothing, so it needs help to read.
- **New feature:** label/highlight the Dipole Repeller as an under-dense "push" region in the flow (an arrow field diverging from a void). Turns "pushed by nothing" into a visible thing.

## Candidate 3
- **Fact:** Even though the universe expands uniformly, our Local Group is swept through space at ~630 km/s (over 2 million km/h) relative to the leftover glow of the Big Bang — driven by distant matter pulling ahead and an empty region pushing behind.
- **Why surprising / lesser-known:** Expansion is pictured as everything drifting calmly apart; on top of that we have a real, measurable fall toward the densest part of our neighbourhood, clocked against the cosmic microwave background.
- **Source:** Hoffman et al., *Nature Astronomy*, 2017 — abstract gives "631±20 km s⁻¹". https://arxiv.org/abs/1702.02483
- **Recency:** 2017
- **Confidence:** high — exact value quoted from the retrieved abstract.
- **On screen:** Inferable — the flow carries our Local Group; the velocity is a number attached to the motion already shown.
- **New feature (optional):** draw our own velocity vector (an arrow from the Local Group pointing along the flow) labelled with the speed.
