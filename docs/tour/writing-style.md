# Tour — writing style

Voice for the on-screen `title` + `narration` at every stage.

## Audience

General public, no astronomy background. Bar: a 12-year-old and their
grandparent both follow it. No jargon without a gloss in the same breath.

## Voice

- **Awe, earned quietly.** Visuals carry the spectacle; words point at it.
  No exclamation marks, no "incredible"/"mind-blowing".
- **Present tense.** "The Milky Way — our home galaxy."
- **Inclusive "our"/"we".** Places the viewer inside the story.
- **One idea per stage.** Don't cram two facts — the next stage takes the
  second.
- **Concrete over abstract.** A number or comparison the body can feel.

## Scale & numbers

- **Light-years in narration, not Mpc** (1 Mpc ≈ 3.26 Mly). Keep Mpc in
  front matter for the engine; speak light-years to the viewer.
- **Round, human numbers**: "2.5 million light-years", "a thousand
  galaxies", "93 billion light-years across". Not "0.78 Mpc".
- **Anchor each rung to the last**: home → neighbours → neighbourhood →
  the structure it sits in.

## Length & timing

- **Title ≤ ~4 words** — a label. "The cosmic web", "You are here".
- **Narration ≤ 2 lines, ~20–30 words** — readable within the dwell. Too
  long → cut words or raise dwell, never rush the reader.

## Don'ts

- No bare jargon ("supercluster" → "a supercluster: thousands of galaxies
  in one structure").
- No second-person commands ("look at…", "notice…") — trust the camera.
- No hype adjectives ("stunning", "epic").
- No precision theatre — no decimals, catalog IDs, or error bars.

## Avoid LLM tells

These constructions instantly read as machine-written. Banned outright:

- **Antithesis / "not just X, but Y".** "Not merely a galaxy, but a city of
  stars." → state the positive claim and stop.
- **The rule-of-three triad.** Parallel nouns/adjectives for rhythm ("vast,
  ancient, and silent"). One strong word beats three.
- **Grandiosity nouns.** No "tapestry", "cosmic dance/ballet", "symphony",
  "testament to", "realm", "expanse", "the wonders of".
- **"Journey / explore / imagine" framing.** Don't narrate the act of
  looking ("Imagine travelling…", "Join us as we explore…") — state the thing.
- **Self-explaining significance.** "A humbling reminder of our place in the
  cosmos." Don't tell the viewer how to feel; the scale does that.
- **Filler verbs.** "home to", "boasts", "nestled", "plays host to". "Virgo
  is home to over a thousand galaxies" → "A thousand galaxies, bound by
  gravity."
- **Hedging.** "perhaps", "some of the", "one of the most" — commit or cut.
- **"From the smallest X to the largest Y".** The tour *is* that; don't say it.
- **Em-dashes.** None. They read as an AI tell. Use a comma, a colon
  (sparingly), or two short sentences instead.
- **Faux-profound fragments.** "Everything. All at once." Write a sentence.

Test each line: would a thoughtful science writer — not a press release, not
a chatbot — write it? If it sounds like a documentary voiceover trying too
hard, cut it back.

## Example (stage 05)

> **The cosmic web**
> Far enough out, galaxies trace a web: bright threads and clumps where
> superclusters gather, ringed by dark voids.

One idea, present tense, no jargon, no imperative, ~18 words, ~6 s read. The
90 Mpc lives in front matter, not the sentence.
