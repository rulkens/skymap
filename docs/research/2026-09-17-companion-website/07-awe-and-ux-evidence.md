# 07 — Awe and UX evidence

Subagent web research, 2026-10-05. Unverified — open the cited source before relying on a claim.

The awe literature supports a quiet, uncluttered, slow, vast-but-safe hero; UX evidence supports a poster-first muted loop with a visible pause, one dominant "Launch" action, and normal scrolling.
Thin or unverified: starfield bitrates, hero-video conversion data, direct evidence on what "kills" awe, institutional-buyer conversion research.

Labels: **[PR]** peer-reviewed · **[STD]** standard/official docs · **[PRAC]** practitioner guidance or opinion · **[UNVERIFIED]** could not confirm, treat as a hypothesis.

## 1. Psychology of awe

| Finding | Source | Design implication |
|---|---|---|
| Awe has two core appraisals: perceived **vastness** and a **need for accommodation** (the stimulus exceeds existing mental frames). Threat, beauty, ability, virtue and the supernatural only "flavour" it. [PR, theoretical] | Keltner & Haidt 2003, *Cognition & Emotion* 17(2):297–314, https://doi.org/10.1080/02699930302297 | The hero must show something larger than the viewer's frame *and* something that needs re-framing: a continuous Earth-to-cosmic-web move does both; a static pretty starfield does only the first. |
| Awe elicitors are information-rich; awe directs attention to the stimulus and away from the self (pride does the opposite). [PR] | Shiota, Keltner & Mossman 2007, *Cognition & Emotion* 21(5):944–963, https://doi.org/10.1080/02699930600923668 | Keep the first screen about the sky, not the maker or the visitor ("you'll love…"). Credentials and the commission pitch go lower. |
| Awe produces a "small self"; nature video (awe vs amusement vs neutral) raised generosity, partly mediated by small self. [PR] A 2023 study did not reproduce the small-self difference in one experiment. | Piff et al. 2015, *JPSP* 108(6):883–899, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25984788/ ; non-replication: https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2023.1097627/full | Short video is an established awe induction, so a loop is a legitimate carrier. Do not promise prosocial or well-being effects in copy. |
| Small-self effect is specific to awe and holds across cultures (6 studies, N = 2,137), varying in size. [PR] | Bai et al. 2017, *JPSP* 113(2):185–209, https://doi.org/10.1037/pspa0000087 | A "you are here" marker that shrinks as the camera pulls back is a direct visual form of the small self. |
| The overview effect (astronauts seeing Earth) is framed as awe plus self-transcendence; authors propose simulated Earth views. [PR, conceptual, not experimental] | Yaden et al. 2016, *Psych. of Consciousness* 3(1):1–11, https://doi.org/10.1037/cns0000086 | Start or end the loop on a recognisable Earth; it is the best-documented single awe image. |
| Immersive (360°) awe video gave higher awe and presence than the same content flat (N = 42). [PR] | Chirico et al. 2017, *Sci. Reports* 7:1218, https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-017-01242-0 | The page video is the weaker medium; its job is to get people into the interactive app, where presence is higher. Supports "Launch" as the single goal. |
| In a science-centre planetarium, more awe raised topic interest but **worsened** accuracy (more false recognition of unstated inferences, poorer error detection). [PR] | Häikiö et al. 2025, *Cognition & Emotion* 39(6):1340–1350, https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02699931.2024.2428787 | Do not put facts you need remembered over the hero. Awe first, information after, in calm sections. |
| VR overview-effect simulation in schools (ages 9–10): awe led to overview effect, which predicted learning gains. [PR] | van Limpt-Broers et al. 2020, *Front. Psychol.* 11:540996, https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.540996 | Usable evidence for the educator section, cited modestly; it is VR, not a web page. |
| Threat-based awe is a distinct variant, with lower well-being and less sense of control. [PR; I read only the citation and secondary summaries] | Gordon et al. 2017, *JPSP* 113(2):310–328, https://doi.org/10.1037/pspp0000120 | Keep vastness threat-free: no ominous score, no "you are insignificant" copy, no vertiginous camera. |
| Awe expanded perceived time and reduced impatience; a later study found no effect on implicit time perception. [PR, contested] | Rudd, Vohs & Aaker 2012, *Psych. Science* 23(10):1130–1136, https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797612438731 ; null: https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13414-019-01924-z | Slow pacing is consistent with the theory, but do not treat "awe makes people linger" as established. |
| Awe video stimuli score much higher on "chills" than merely positive clips (fMRI study pretest). [PR] | https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6766853 | Chills depend on build and release; a 10–20 s loop needs one clear reveal, not uniform drift. |

Gaps and cautions:
- **Music.** Chirico & Gaggioli 2019 ("When Silence Is Better Than Thousands Sounds", https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-030-25872-6_1) tested VR with and without music; the page is paywalled and I could not read the result, so I draw no conclusion from it. The title suggests sound does not always help.
- **What kills awe.** I found no experiment that manipulates clutter, interruption or sales messaging and measures awe. The implication (strip the hero, no pop-ups, no selling above the fold) is an inference from the attention-to-stimulus and small-self findings above, not a tested result.
- Most inductions are lab studies on students with self-report measures; effect sizes on a web page are unknown.

## 2. Conveying cosmic scale

- **Powers of Ten** (Eames, 1977; National Film Registry 1998): one continuous move, a tenfold wider view every ten seconds, starting from a human on a picnic blanket; derived from Kees Boeke's *Cosmic View* (1957). https://www.eamesoffice.com/the-work/powers-of-ten/ , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powers_of_Ten_(film_series) [PRAC; canonical exemplar, no audience study found]. Implication: constant *logarithmic* rate, human-scale start, an on-screen scale readout.
- **Accuracy collapses away from human scale.** 215 participants from fifth grade to doctoral level judged sizes best near human scale. Tretter, Jones & Minogue 2006, *J. Res. Sci. Teaching* 43(10):1061–1085, https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ760172 [PR]. Implication: anchor every step to the previous one, not to metres.
- **Number numbness.** About half of adults place 1 million near the middle of a thousand-to-billion line, treating scale words as equally spaced. Landy, Silbert & Goldin 2013, *Cognitive Science* 37(5):775–799, https://doi.org/10.1111/cogs.12028 [PR]. Implication: "2.5 million galaxies" and "13.8 billion years" as bare stats convey little; avoid a stat-counter row.
- **Compression bias and a fix.** People overestimate small and underestimate large magnitudes; "hierarchical alignment" (nesting each scale inside the next) improved estimates. Resnick, Newcombe & Shipley 2017, *Cognitive Science* 41(4):1020–1041, https://doi.org/10.1111/cogs.12388 [PR]. Implication: a nested ladder (Earth, Solar System, Milky Way, Local Group, cosmic web), each rung shown inside the next.
- **Re-expressed numbers help.** "Perspectives" (ratios, ranks, unit changes) improved recall, estimation and error detection in three randomized experiments, N > 3,200. Barrio, Goldstein & Hofman, CHI 2016, https://doi.org/10.1145/2858036.2858510 [PR]. Implication: one comparison sentence per number ("light from here left before…").
- **Scale collapse in diagrams.** Textbook images compress the Earth–Moon gap by a median factor of 23; student drawings match. Cole et al. 2018, *Phys. Rev. PER*, https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.14.010139 [PR]. Students' distance estimates run low and scatter over orders of magnitude (Miller & Brewer 2010, https://www.researchgate.net/publication/248975438_Misconceptions_of_Astronomical_Distances). Implication: "true scale, one scene" is the real differentiator; say it plainly and show it.
- **Practitioner note.** Ryan Wyatt (Morrison Planetarium) on having to "cheat" scale within a show's runtime: https://www.calacademy.org/explore-science/thoughts-from-a-planetarian-visualizing-numbers [PRAC].
- I found no empirical study of audience comprehension of continuous zooms in fulldome; a likely lead I did not read is Jensen, Borkiewicz & Naiman 2022, *Frontiers in Communication*, doi 10.3389/fcomm.2022.840631.

## 3. UX evidence by pattern

**Autoplaying hero video**
- WCAG 2.2.2 (Level A): content that moves, starts automatically, lasts over 5 s and sits beside other content needs a pause, stop or hide mechanism. https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html [STD]. A 10–20 s loop is in scope; a visible pause button is mandatory.
- NN/g "Video Usability": unrequested playback is poorly received; anything moving pulls attention from the task. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/video-usability/ [PRAC, based on user testing]. It does not address decorative background video specifically.
- web.dev lists autoplaying video among motion triggers and documents `prefers-reduced-motion`. https://web.dev/articles/prefers-reduced-motion [STD]. Under reduced motion, show the poster only.
- Chrome: "Muted autoplay is always allowed"; "Don't assume a video will play." https://developer.chrome.com/blog/autoplay [STD]. iOS Low Power Mode is reported to block even muted autoplay (Apple forum thread https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/727855; third-party sources) [UNVERIFIED in Apple docs]. The poster must stand alone as the complete hero.
- Conversion and comprehension effects of hero video: I found no credible controlled study. Drop any such statistic.

**Scroll-driven animation and scrolljacking**
- NN/g usability testing: "The majority of our study participants were at least mildly disoriented by scrolljacking"; worst when altered scroll rate is combined with text to read; worse on mobile; task-focused users least tolerant. Acceptable only below the fold, without direction changes, with little text, alongside normal sections. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolljacking-101/ [PRAC, qualitative study].
- Implication: scroll-*linked* effects that never alter scroll speed or direction are fine (a scale ladder that advances with the page); never hijack the wheel. Institutional buyers are the task-focused group.

**Display type over imagery**
- WCAG 1.4.3 (AA): 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for large text (18 pt, or 14 pt bold). https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html [STD]. Contrast must hold against the brightest frame of the loop behind the text.
- NN/g "Ensure High Contrast for Text Over Images": use scrims, overlays or a text-safe region. https://www.nngroup.com/articles/text-over-images/ [PRAC]. A dark starfield helps, but a bright Earth limb or galaxy core passing behind a headline breaks it; reserve a text-safe zone in the camera path.

**Dark themes**
- For normal vision, light mode "leads to better performance most of the time" (smaller pupil, less spherical aberration); the advantage grows as font size shrinks (Piepenbrock et al. 2013); dark may suit people with cloudy ocular media (Legge et al. 1985). Summary by Budiu 2020, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode/ [PRAC summary of PR studies; I did not read the primaries].
- That article does **not** discuss halation or astigmatism; the widespread attribution is wrong. Halation and "use off-white on dark grey" are practitioner guidance: Material Design 2 recommends a #121212 surface and 87% white text, https://m2.material.io/design/color/dark-theme.html [PRAC]. WCAG sets no contrast ceiling.
- Implication: dark is right for the subject, but pay the cost with larger body text, generous line height, short paragraphs, near-black surfaces and off-white text.

**One CTA versus several; three audiences**
- NN/g advises against audience-based navigation: people fit several groups or none, and cannot tell "about" from "for". https://www.nngroup.com/articles/audience-based-navigation/ [PRAC, research-based].
- Single primary CTA: consistent practitioner advice (one dominant action, secondary demoted to an outline or text link), but I found no NN/g or peer-reviewed source. [PRAC, thin].
- Implication: do not gate the page with "I am a visitor / educator / institution". One "Launch" button for everyone; task-named sections lower down ("Use it in a classroom", "Commission a build"), each with its own quieter link.

**Sound**
- WCAG 1.4.2 (A): audio that autoplays over 3 s needs a pause or independent volume control; W3C discourages autoplaying sound. https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/audio-control.html [STD]. Browsers block unmuted autoplay anyway (Chrome doc above).
- Implication: silent by default; if there is a score, an explicit labelled toggle. The music evidence for awe (section 1) is too weak to justify more.

**First impression**
- Visual-appeal ratings formed at 50 ms agree with those at 500 ms. Lindgaard et al. 2006, *Behaviour & IT* 25(2):115–126, https://doi.org/10.1080/01449290500330448 [PR; I read secondary summaries].
- Low visual complexity and high prototypicality give the best first impressions, including at 50 ms exposure. Tuch et al. 2012, *IJHCS* 70(11):794–811, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2012.06.003 [PR].
- Perceived colourfulness and complexity plus demographics explain about 48% of variance in appeal after 500 ms (450 sites). Reinecke et al., CHI 2013, https://doi.org/10.1145/2470654.2481281 [PR].
- Implication: the judgement is made on the **poster frame**, before any video plays. Art-direct that still as the real hero. Keep the layout conventional (logo top-left, nav top-right, headline, one button); spend novelty on the image, not the structure.

## 4. Performance for a video-led hero

- **LCP.** `<video>` is an LCP candidate: "the poster image load time or first frame presentation time for videos is used—whichever is earlier." Good is 2.5 s or less at p75. https://web.dev/articles/lcp [STD].
- **Poster priority.** `poster` has no priority attribute of its own; web.dev recommends `<link rel="preload" as="image" href="poster…" fetchpriority="high">`. https://web.dev/learn/performance/video-performance , https://web.dev/articles/lazy-loading-video (updated 2 July 2026) [STD].
- **Do not lazy-load the hero.** Native `loading="lazy"` on `<video>`/`<audio>` shipped in Chrome 148 (5 May 2026; https://developer.chrome.com/release-notes/148). web.dev: "Videos that are LCP elements shouldn't use `loading="lazy"`". Use it for below-fold clips; other engines' support unconfirmed.
- **Attributes.** `autoplay muted loop playsinline`; strip the audio track (`-an`). web.dev video-performance page [STD]. If the poster is preloaded, `preload="none"` plus a script that attaches sources after first paint keeps video bytes off the critical path [PRAC; my composition of the documented pieces].
- **Source order.** The browser takes the first `<source>` it can play; list modern codecs first with a `codecs` string. Safari plays AV1 only on hardware-decode devices (A17 Pro, M3 and later), so include HEVC (`hvc1`) or accept H.264 there. https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Media/Guides/Formats/codecs_parameter , https://bitmovin.com/blog/av1-playback-support/ [STD/PRAC]. Suggested order: AV1, then HEVC `hvc1`, then H.264.
- **Dark starfield compression.** 10-bit encoding reduces banding in dark areas; AV1 film-grain synthesis can mask it at low bitrate. https://gitlab.com/AOMediaCodec/SVT-AV1/-/blob/master/Docs/CommonQuestions.md , https://gist.github.com/dvaupel/716598fc9e7c2d436b54ae00f7a34b95 [PRAC]. **I found no source giving bitrates for starfield loops.** My unverified starting guess for 1080p, 15 s: AV1 roughly 1–2.5 Mbps, H.264 roughly 4–6 Mbps; test on the real footage, adding slight dither or grain before encoding and judging on a phone OLED.
- **`<video>` versus animated AVIF/WebP.** web.dev: "You should generally avoid animated image formats"; video is more efficient. Same page [STD].
- **Cloudflare.** Stream bills $5 per 1,000 minutes stored and $1 per 1,000 minutes delivered (https://developers.cloudflare.com/stream/pricing); R2 has zero egress fees and needs a custom domain to sit behind the cache. A pre-encoded short loop needs no adaptive ladder, so R2 or plain static assets fit better. R2 range-request behaviour and cacheable-size limits: not confirmed (check https://developers.cloudflare.com/cache/concepts/default-cache-behavior/). R2 unit prices came from third-party posts.
- **View Transitions for the handoff.** Cross-document `@view-transition { navigation: auto }` requires same origin and opt-in on both pages; MDN lists it as "Limited availability" (https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Reference/At-rules/@view-transition). Secondary sources put support at Chrome/Edge 126+ and Safari 18.2+, with Firefox stable lacking the cross-document form (same-document shipped in Firefox 144); sources conflict and I could not read the compat table. Unsupported browsers navigate normally, so it is safe as enhancement; wrap in `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference` (https://css-tricks.com/cross-document-view-transitions-part-1/). If the app lives on another origin or subdomain, it will not fire.
- **CSS scroll-driven animations.** caniuse for `animation-timeline: scroll()`, read today: Chrome/Edge 115+, Safari and iOS Safari 26.0+, Firefox from 160 (earlier versions unsupported; Firefox for Android unsupported), global 87.22%. https://caniuse.com/mdn-css_properties_animation-timeline_scroll . Firefox 160 appears to be ahead of current stable (my inference from version cadence). Use behind `@supports (animation-timeline: scroll())` with a static fallback.

## 5. Commission page for institutional buyers

Evidence here is thin: sector guidance and vendor opinion, no conversion research specific to museums or planetariums.

- **Proof.** AAM vendor-collaboration post (written by a print vendor's CEO, so opinion): "A strong portfolio that includes museum-specific projects is a good indicator"; case studies, referrals from other museums, visiting completed installations, fast and thorough responses. https://www.aam-us.org/2025/04/04/less-stress-more-success-the-ultimate-tips-for-vendor-collaboration/ [PRAC]. Implication: named installs (the Wisdome dome fisheye work is exactly this), photos of the thing running in a venue, a named reference contact, a stated response time.
- **Procurement.** Museums choose between an open RFP, an invited RFP and an RFQ; an RFP forces them to know budget, scope and date first. Punt, "To RFP or Not to RFP", AAM *Exhibition*, Spring 2019, https://www.aam-us.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/12_Exhibition_ToRFPOrNotTORFP.pdf [PRAC]. AAM's ethical-contracting piece says demanding detailed plans at proposal stage is asking for free work: https://www.aam-us.org/2021/09/10/ethical-contracting-is-your-museum-doing-it/ . AAM sets no sole-source threshold; thresholds come from the institution or its parent body (my general knowledge, not sourced). Implication: provide what a staff member needs to justify a small direct purchase or to name you in an RFQ: a one-page capability PDF, formats delivered (fulldome fisheye, kiosk, offline build), licence terms, legal entity and invoicing details.
- **Planetarium buying habits.** Licence fees scale with attendance, seats and dome size; terms run from one year to decades; small domes are price-sensitive (a director cited $5,000 a year as about 600 tickets for a 60-seat dome). https://www.domefestwest.com/post/fulldome-distribution-what-buyers-want , Clark Planetarium 2021 price list https://slco.legistar.com/gateway.aspx?M=F&ID=9bbd92b7-5623-4592-9f9a-e88936eb2c34.pdf , IPS vendor directory and Fulldome Database https://www.ips-planetarium.org/page/resources [PRAC, partly dated]. Implication: say which dome systems and resolutions you deliver, offer a screener or evaluation build, and consider listing in IPS and fddb, where buyers already look.
- **Contact form.** Baymard's research is on checkout, not lead forms: average 12.8 fields against a workable 6–8, and an unexplained required phone field erodes trust. https://baymard.com/blog/checkout-optimization-from-16-fields-to-8 , https://baymard.com/blog/explain-phone-number-field [PRAC, large-scale testing, different context]. CXL notes fewer fields is a heuristic and can lower B2B lead quality: https://cxl.com/blog/form-design-best-practices/ . B2B field-count statistics attributed to HubSpot, Formstack and NN/g circulate in blogs; I could not trace them and have dropped them. Implication: name, institution, email, free-text message; optional venue type and timeline; no phone; also show a plain email address, since some institutional users cannot or will not use forms.
- **Spam.** Cloudflare Turnstile avoids visual puzzles and states "Turnstile is WCAG 2.2 AA compliant". https://developers.cloudflare.com/turnstile/ [STD, vendor claim]. Pricing and server-side verification details were not on the page I read.
- Freelancer and studio marketing research: nothing credible found.

## Ten best-supported implications, ranked

1. Give the hero loop a visible, keyboard-reachable pause control and replace it with the still under `prefers-reduced-motion`. WCAG 2.2.2, https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html
2. Treat the poster frame as the real hero and preload it at high priority, because it is both the LCP element and the image the 50 ms judgement is made on. https://web.dev/articles/lcp ; Lindgaard et al. 2006, https://doi.org/10.1080/01449290500330448
3. Keep the layout conventional and visually simple and let the footage carry the novelty. Tuch et al. 2012, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2012.06.003
4. Never alter scroll speed or direction; restrict scroll-linked effects to below the fold with little text. NN/g, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolljacking-101/
5. Show vastness that needs re-framing: one continuous human-to-cosmic move with a single reveal, not ambient drift. Keltner & Haidt 2003, https://doi.org/10.1080/02699930302297
6. Keep facts off the hero and put them in calm sections afterwards, since awe raises interest but lowers accuracy. Häikiö et al. 2025, https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/02699931.2024.2428787
7. Convey scale with a nested ladder and one human comparison per number, never a row of bare large statistics. Resnick et al. 2017, https://doi.org/10.1111/cogs.12388 ; Landy et al. 2013, https://doi.org/10.1111/cogs.12028
8. Do not route visitors by audience label; one "Launch" action for all, with task-named sections lower down. NN/g, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/audience-based-navigation/
9. Guarantee 4.5:1 text contrast against the brightest frame behind it via a text-safe zone or scrim, and offset the dark theme with larger off-white body type. WCAG 1.4.3, https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/contrast-minimum.html ; Budiu 2020, https://www.nngroup.com/articles/dark-mode/
10. Ship silent, and use view transitions and scroll-driven CSS strictly as enhancement behind feature detection, since Firefox stable lacks both as of today. WCAG 1.4.2, https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/audio-control.html ; https://caniuse.com/mdn-css_properties_animation-timeline_scroll

Not ranked because the evidence is too thin: anything about hero-video conversion, music for awe, exact bitrates, and commission-page conversion.
