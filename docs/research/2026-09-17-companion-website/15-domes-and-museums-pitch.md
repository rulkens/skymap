# 15 - How dome and museum technology is pitched

Subagent study, 2026-10-30, branch `site/15-homepage`, for the redesign of the Domes and museums page (`/home/domes/`). Evidence key as in note 12: **[V]** page fetched this session through a summarising model · **[S]** search summary only · **[J]** my judgement. No vendor page was seen as pixels; the fetches describe structure and words, not look.

## Who sells what, and how

- **Cosm / Evans & Sutherland, Digistar.** The page runs headline → feature highlights with pictures → data updates → uses → three case studies → CTA, with "Get Digistar", "Book Digistar Demo" and "Contact Us" at the top, middle and foot [V https://tech.cosm.com/digistar]. The claims are specific: real-time access to Gaia and other surveys, 72-bit processing "to eliminate banding in dark astronomical scenes", auto-align and auto-blend of projectors, a cloud library shared by 500+ sites [S https://www.cosm.com/news/cosm-unveils-the-latest-version-of-digistar-advancing-immersive-learning-and-global-connection-for-the-science-and-education-community]. The proof is named installations with projector counts and resolutions (Peoria: six projectors at 6.5K; Shanghai: 20 projectors at 14K+) [S https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/cosm-companies-evans--sutherland--spitz-inc-help-power-experience-at-the-new-shanghai-astronomy-museum-with-industry-leading-technology-design-and-engineering-expertise-301339110.html].
- **Sky-Skan, DigitalSky Dark Matter.** Sold as one system for simulation, fulldome video playback, show control and lighting ("say goodbye to separate computers") [S https://skyskan.com/products/digital-planetarium-systems/]. Its users wrote their own documentation on GitHub because the vendor's was thin [S https://github.com/zaida04/digitalsky-dark-matter-documentation], so readable documentation is itself an argument.
- **ZEISS, UNIVIEW.** Section titles name capabilities ("Real-time storytelling", "True Hybrid control", "Expand your dome"). The pictures show what appears on the dome, not the interface. The key promise is the presenter's freedom: "the freedom to respond spontaneously to questions", "adapt the story as it unfolds". It ends on a contact form and a user group [V https://www.zeiss.com/planetariums/en/products-and-solutions/software-and-applications.html]. Directors are quoted by name and title [S https://www.zeiss.com/planetariums/us/newsroom/news-overview/2022-news/bremen-planetarium-relies-on-uniview.html].
- **OpenSpace (AMNH, Linköping).** Open source and free; drives the Hayden's monthly live shows; "over 80 institutions" [S https://www.ppadomes.org/events/online-seminars/ppsarchive/2025-archive/2025-01-31-chat]. Live dome use needs "a high-end laptop and a trained operator". The Houston Museum of Natural Science is building an interface so "the operator can become the presenter" in the dark theatre [S https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2024AGUFMED22B..04R/abstract]. Source and requirements are public [S https://github.com/OpenSpace/OpenSpace].
- **ESO Supernova.** "The world's first open source planetarium": full shows and 450 fulldome clips, free under Creative Commons with credit [S https://supernova.eso.org/news/releases/es1901/]. The page for planetariums is a plain table of title, summary, producer and running time, and the formats are on a separate page [V https://supernova.eso.org/for-planetariums/]. Licence and credit are the first thing it settles.
- **Museum studios.** Local Projects prototypes before it builds: "upwards of a dozen concepts in full", shown to stakeholders so they "buy into it" [V https://www.core77.com/posts/26547/design-indaba-conference-2014-jake-barton-of-local-projects-on-his-prototype-intensive-process-26547]. Gallery One had six weeks of testing between the stakeholder opening and the public one [S https://mw2013.museumsandtheweb.com/paper/transforming-the-art-museum-experience-gallery-one-2/index.html]. Ideum sells its process as named phases: a foundations workshop, visioning, then implementation planning with timelines, milestones and "preliminary cost analyses" [V https://ideum.com/news/ideums-leadership-in-exhibition-concept-design]. Case studies name the curators they wrote the content with [S https://ideum.com/portfolio/rfid-table].

## What a director needs to say yes

1. **The format, stated in numbers.** The common master is a 4096 × 4096 domemaster at 30 fps, a fisheye of 180° (210° for domes that reach the floor), with the zenith at the centre and the front of the theatre at the bottom [S https://fulldomefever.com/glossary/domemaster.html]. Distributors ask for numbered frames (JPG or PNG) and 48 kHz WAV audio [V https://www.fulldomeondemand.com/producers.html]; some playback systems take H.264 or H.265 directly [S https://vioso.com/images/vioso/solutions/VIOSO-Fulldome-Solutions-22.pdf]. NOIRLab offers each show as a 1K preview, 2K and 4K playback files, and a 4K or 8K master to slice [S https://noirlab.edu/public/video-formats/].
2. **Proof on their own dome.** "The single highest-leverage move is getting eyes on a 10-second test sequence in an actual dome" [V https://aetherimmersive.com/blog/designing-for-curved-surfaces-fulldome/]. Distributors give free clips and three-day rentals for exactly this [S https://www.fulldomeondemand.com/shows_bonus.html]. The dome system slices the master between projectors and blends the overlaps; the content's job is to give it a clean disc [V Aether, above; S https://www.lochnessproductions.com/reference/primer/primer.html].
3. **Live or pre-rendered.** Live shows are argued for as "a conversation", and the deciding point is control in the hand: "complete control of the system was available in my hand", not "tied down to a physical console" [V https://www.digitaliseducation.com/blog-post-20190711.en.html]. The same author measured training time (under an hour against about five). A film's argument is that it does not depend on the theatre's computer.
4. **Licences and credit.** Even free shows can need a permission form before screening [S https://eplanetarium.com/shows_bundled.php]. ESO's whole offer rests on stating the licence once and plainly [S https://www.eso.org/public/announcements/ann14051/].
5. **Process and cost.** Phases with named deliverables, a prototype or test before the build, and a cost estimate before commitment (Ideum, Local Projects, above).
6. **References.** Vendors name venues, projector counts and directors. Skymap may not name venues on its site (owner's ruling), so its references must be things a director can check alone: the open code, the documentation, and a link that opens the live view.

## What makes these pages convincing and beautiful

- The dome's own picture leads, not a photograph of the hardware or the interface (ZEISS, Cosm) [V].
- Numbers in a specification list, set as data (resolution, frame rate, projector count) [V Cosm; J].
- One call to action, repeated at the top, middle and foot (Cosm) [V].
- The presenter's freedom is shown, not described: a tablet or a phone in the room (ZEISS, OpenSpace / HMNS) [V, S].
- [J] The ESO table shows that a plain page with complete facts persuades this audience more than an animated one: these readers are technicians and educators.

## What this means for Skymap's Domes page

- **Opening: show the frame as a domemaster.** Put the real fisheye render first and mark it the way a technician reads one: the zenith at the centre, the front at the bottom edge, the horizon on the rim. This is the single bold idea; it answers "will this run in my dome" before any words do.
- **"Live or as a film" as a specification list.** Two columns side by side (live and film) with rows a director scans: picture, size, frame rate, file, sound, who steers it. Skymap's numbers match the industry master (4,096 × 4,096 equidistant fisheye at 30 or 60 fps). Say plainly what differs: the recorder writes H.264 in MP4 and no sound, not numbered frames, and slicing and blending are the dome system's job.
- **Steer the dome from a phone** is Skymap's version of "the operator becomes the presenter" (HMNS) and "control in my hand" (Cuomo). It keeps its demo and goes higher on the page, straight after the specification.
- **The exhibit screen** is a second offer, not the first: one picture, the notes readable standing up, the kiosk mode stated as planned work.
- **Licences go before the process, short and strict:** Gaia and EOX imagery are non-commercial, and that affects paid dome work. Say it once and link the credits page, as ESO does.
- **"How a commission runs"** keeps its four steps, with the test on the director's own dome as the step that matters (Aether's "10-second test sequence").
- **The open code and the documentation stand in for named references**, since no venue is named.
- **End on the contact form**, the page's one call to action, which the opening's link also points to.
