## Astronomy and space

- **NASA JPL Education.** Its lesson cards show type, subject and grade range. The filters add time ("Under 30 mins" up to "Over 2 hrs") and a "Quick and Easy" set. Lessons open on a question about a real mission or world: "Why are the tallest peaks in the solar system found on one of its smallest worlds?" [V] https://www.jpl.nasa.gov/edu/teach/
- **ESA Teach with Space.** Each resource gives a narrow age band (8–12, 14–16), a teacher guide with pupil activities, and on some resources a time per activity ("30 minutes per activity"). The page opens "In brief" and has an "In-depth" section after it. [S] https://www.esa.int/Education/Teachers_Corner/Solar_System_and_Universe · https://www.esa.int/Education/Teachers_Corner/A_week_in_the_life_of_an_astronaut_Teach_with_space_PR57
- **IAU astroEDU.** Activities are peer reviewed by a scientist and an educator, and every activity follows one template: materials, goals, objectives, background, numbered steps, evaluation, and curriculum links. [S] https://astroedu.iau.org/en/about/ · https://www.astro4edu.org/resources/
- **OpenStax Astronomy 2e.** Every chapter opens with "Thinking Ahead": a question before any teaching. Ch. 2 asks how you would prove to a flat-Earth neighbour that Earth is a sphere. Ch. 19 asks "How large is the universe? What is the most distant object we can see?" [S] https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/2-thinking-ahead · https://openstax.org/books/astronomy-2e/pages/19-thinking-ahead
- **Night Sky Network toolkits.** Their premise is that distance and scale are "two of the most misunderstood ideas in astronomy". The Pocket Solar System is a paper strip the length of a pupil. It gets an "Ah-ha!" because the outer solar system is so empty and the inner orbits so close together. [S] https://nightsky.jpl.nasa.gov/download-list.cfm?Category=ToolKit:+Our+Galaxy,+Our+Universe · https://community.starnetlibraries.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/02/Pocket-solar-system.pdf
- **Stellarium in teaching.** The K–8 guides run 15–30 minutes and need one computer and a projector, as a class demo or in small groups. They tell the teacher to set the place first. Science North's grade 6 lesson uses the browser version and has a backup for rooms without internet: answer from the pictures provided. [S] https://fieldsacademy.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Stellarium-Teacher.pdf · https://www.sciencenorth.ca/sites/default/files/2018/April%2022%20Grade%206%20Stellarium%201%20ENG.pdf
- **WorldWide Telescope.** Teachers and astronomers make scripted "tours" for classes and play them back. The tours are also the unit of sharing. [S] https://worldwidetelescope.org/use/everyone/ · https://wwtambassadors.org/publications/wwt-ambassadors-worldwide-telescope-interactive-learning-0
- **Zooniverse in class.** Interest rose sharply once pupils knew their classifications helped real scientists (NSTA, wildlife cameras). The tasks are short enough for primary pupils. [S] https://www.nsta.org/science-teacher/science-teacher-mayjune-2021/exploring-zooniverse · https://www.physics.ox.ac.uk/engage/teachers/secondary-schools/zooniverse-schools

## Other subjects

- **PhET simulations.** The sims use implicit scaffolding, "guides students without feeling guided". Two tips for teachers:
  - start with an open "play with this and record what you discover";
  - give challenge prompts instead of step instructions ("not: set the angle to 45 degrees").

  In the study, 80 chemistry students got ten minutes and no instructions, and still explored productively. [S] https://www.aapt.org/Conferences/newfaculty/upload/Vieyra-NFW-Introduction-to-PhET-Interactive-Simulations.pdf · https://www.per-central.org/items/detail.cfm?ID=16328 · https://www.physport.org/recommendations/Entry.cfm?ID=93341
- **Desmos activity design.** The guide's main rules:
  - "fewer screens and inputs are better than more";
  - vary the verbs: predict, estimate, compare, argue, not only calculate;
  - "easy to start, hard to finish";
  - many ways to be right;
  - informal before formal;
  - delay feedback.

  [S] https://blog.desmos.com/articles/the-desmos-guide-to-building-great-digital-math/ · https://blog.mrmeyer.com/2017/desmos-design-why-were-suspicious-of-immediate-feedback/
- **Three-act tasks (Dan Meyer).** Act one shows a picture or film and asks as little as possible: "leave no one out". In act two pupils guess, then work out what they need to know. Act three reveals the answer with the same kind of picture. [S] https://blog.mrmeyer.com/2011/the-three-acts-of-a-mathematical-story/
- **3Blue1Brown.** "It's easiest to remember a piece of math if you feel like you invented it yourself." The method is visuals first and a concrete example before the abstraction. [S] https://3blue1brown.substack.com/p/some4-prizes · https://stanforddaily.com/2020/01/24/3blue1brown-creator-grant-sanderson-15-talks-engaging-with-math-using-stories-and-visuals/
- **Notice and wonder (Annie Fetter, Math Forum).** Project a picture with no question and ask "What do you notice? What do you wonder?". Pupils look alone first, then compare. There are no wrong answers, so everyone can start. [S] https://annie.mathematicalthinking.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/FetterSDSTEM2020F507.pdf · https://ii.library.jhu.edu/2018/05/21/notice-and-wonder/
- **Predict, observe, explain (White & Gunstone 1992).** Pupils predict with a reason, observe, then reconcile the two. The prediction exposes what they believed before. [S] https://www.researchgate.net/publication/265847902_Predict_Observe_Explain_Activities_enhancing_scientific_understanding
- **The BSCS 5E model.** The order is engage, explore, explain, elaborate, evaluate. Engage is an event pupils cannot yet explain. Explaining before exploring weakens the model. [S] https://media.bscs.org/bscsmw/5es/bscs_5e_full_report.pdf · https://www.sdcoe.net/ngss/evidence-based-practices/5e-model-of-instruction
- **Anchoring phenomena (NGSS, STEM Teaching Tools 28 and 42).** A good phenomenon is observable and connects to pupils' lives. It is too rich to explain in one lesson and does not need to be dramatic. Pupils should not be able to look the answer up. [S] https://stemteachingtools.org/assets/landscapes/STEM-Teaching-Tool-28-Qualities-of-Anchor-Phenomena.pdf · https://stemteachingtools.org/assets/landscapes/STEM-Teaching-Tool-42_Using_Phenomena_in_NGSS_23.pdf
- **Exploratorium Science Snacks.** Each snack gives a time ("15 minutes or more") and has two parts: "To Do and Notice" (steps that end with what to look for) and "What's Going On?" (the explanation, kept apart). [S] https://www.exploratorium.edu/snacks/about
- **Project-based learning (PBLWorks).** Its elements start from "a challenging problem or question", phrased as an open-ended driving question, and include student voice and choice and a public product. [S] https://www.pblworks.org/what-is-pbl/gold-standard-project-design
- **Retrieval practice (Agarwal).** End on an exit ticket: one question answered from memory, short answer, low stakes, alone. It "can be done in as little as one minute". [S] https://pdf.retrievalpractice.org/RetrievalPracticeGuide.pdf · https://www.ascd.org/blogs/retrieval-practice-one-minute-to-better-student-learning

## What makes a lesson engaging, across all of them

1. **A hook pupils can see.** Something observable they cannot yet explain: a picture, a film, a thing at true scale. It does not need to be dramatic (NGSS, 5E, three acts).
2. **The question comes before the explanation.** It is asked first, and the answer is found, not read (OpenStax, JPL, 5E, POE).
3. **Guess first.** A written guess makes the observation matter and shows the teacher what pupils believed (POE, three acts, Desmos).
4. **Pupils choose where to go.** Open exploration with a challenge is better than step instructions. Many ways to be right (PhET, Desmos, PBL).
5. **Scale is felt, not stated.** The "Ah-ha" comes from seeing the empty space between things (Night Sky Network).
6. **A short setup for the teacher.** Time, who works (whole class, pairs, alone), what is needed (a projector, one screen per pair), then one link. Stellarium lessons need a computer and a projector. Fewer inputs win (JPL, ESA, Desmos).
7. **The explanation is kept apart.** "To Do and Notice" before "What's Going On?" (Exploratorium).
8. **End on one question from memory** (retrieval practice).
9. **Ask how we know.** Evidence is part of the lesson: real data matters to pupils (Zooniverse, NGSS).

## What this means for Skymap's Classroom page

**Structure.** The page does what it describes: it opens on a question the reader can see, then shows how to run a lesson, then which links to give, then the setup.

1. **Opening: a question at true scale.** Earth and the Moon side by side at true scale, clean on black, with the question "How many Earths would fit between them?". The answer is kept apart, behind a reveal (findings 1, 2, 5, 7).
2. **How to run a lesson.** Each lesson format is a named routine from the research. Its meta line gives who, what is needed and how long, then the routine in two or three sentences, an example question and the link (finding 6):
   - *Notice and wonder*, on the tour, with the whole class at the projector (findings 1, 2);
   - *Guess, then look*, on the clock: pupils write a guess and then run it (finding 3);
   - *Find it yourselves*, in pairs on their own screens, with a challenge in place of instructions (finding 4);
   - *Ask how we know*, on an exhibit's notes, sorting what is on screen into measured, derived and drawn (finding 9).
   The section closes on an exit question answered without a screen (finding 8).
3. **Questions to start with.** The ready-made links, question first. The question is set in the display face and the view's name is the small label, grouped by curriculum topic like JPL's filters (findings 2, 6).
4. **Make your own link.** Three steps, each shown with the app's own control.
5. **Before the lesson.** Try one link on a pupil's device, what it runs on, and the limits (finding 6).

**Copy patterns.**
- A question pupils answer by going there and looking: "How many times wider is Neptune's orbit than Mercury's?". Never "read the number off the card".
- One verb per format, in the imperative: "Pause", "Guess", "Find", "Sort".
- A meta line in plain words: "The whole class, one projector, five minutes".
- The answer is never in the question's sentence. Where the page gives one, it is behind a reveal.
