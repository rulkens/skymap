# Supernovae as a Structuring Agent of the Dusty ISM — Literature Review

Context: what a model built from (a) a Gerola–Seiden-style SSPSF cellular automaton driving arm dust placement, and (b) a ~125-event discrete SF-event catalog that carves dust "bubbles" as `age^2.5`-grown negative splats, structurally omits by never representing supernovae as discrete, located, timed explosions with their own physics.

---

## 1. Single-SN remnants: size evolution, lifetimes, visible population

**LITERATURE, verified (partial — search-synthesized, not full-text read)** / **SECONDARY** for most of this section; the general SNR evolutionary staging is textbook physics repeated consistently across every source pulled, so it is treated as well-established even though no single paper was read cover-to-cover.

Canonical staging (Cioffi, McKee & Bertschinger 1988; Sedov-Taylor solution):

- **Free expansion**: ~100 yr, until swept-up mass exceeds ejecta mass.
- **Sedov-Taylor (adiabatic)**: begins ~10³ yr, lasts to a few×10⁴ yr. `R(t) ≈ 31.5 E51^0.2 n0^-0.2 t5^0.4` pc — a _decelerating_ power law.
- **Snowplough (radiative)**: cooling time drops below age; `R(t) ≈ 27.9 E51^0.221 n0^-0.257 t5^0.3` pc, up to ~5×10⁵ yr. Exponent flattens further (0.3) — continued deceleration.
- **Merger with ISM**: the shell's internal pressure drops to ambient ISM pressure and the remnant dissolves as a distinct shell. McKee & Ostriker (1977) tie the merger radius to porosity/pressure equilibrium with the hot ionized medium rather than giving one fixed number; commonly cited (**RECALLED**, not independently verified this session) final radii before merger are of order **50–100 pc**, reached at ages of roughly **1–2×10⁵ yr**.

**SN rate anchor for an M74-like galaxy (SECONDARY)**: NGC 628 has SFR ≈ 0.7 M☉/yr (Calzetti et al. 2010, via search synthesis). Standard SFR→core-collapse-rate scalings (~1 CCSN per century per ~2 M☉/yr of SFR, Milky-Way-calibrated) imply roughly **1 CCSN every 150–300 yr** for M74 from theory. Contrast with the _observed_ rate: **4 SNe recorded in NGC 628 between 2002 and 2019** (2002ap, 2003gd, 2013ej, AT2019krl) — an observed cadence of roughly 1 every 4–6 years over the modern-survey era (**LITERATURE, verified** — fetched summary of Michałowski et al. 2020 via search, cross-checked against the Messier SNe list). [The theoretical rate and the survey-era detection cadence are not directly comparable; the theoretical number is the physical anchor.]

Given the ST/snowplough timescale (≤5×10⁵ yr) and a galaxy-wide CCSN rate of order 1 per 150–300 yr, the expected **standing population of individually-resolvable single-SN remnants at any instant** is on the order of a few hundred to ~2000 across a whole disk the size of M74 — small compared to the disk area, but they are the mechanism that populates the ISM with **small (parsec-to-few-tens-of-pc), short-lived, decelerating cavities**, distinct in size and kinematics from the multi-SN superbubbles (§4) that dominate at larger scale.

**Assessment**: The model's discrete SF-event catalog only ever carves "bubbles" tied to _clustered_ star-formation events aged via `age^2.5`, and only on arm ridges. It has no mechanism at all for the much more numerous **single, isolated, short-lived SNR population** — the small holes with lifetimes of ~10⁵ yr that are the dominant contributor to _small-scale_ cavity texture at every location in the disk, not just arm ridges. Capturing this requires new machinery: a population of small, short-lived, radius-decelerating cavities seeded stochastically (not tied to the arm SF-event catalog) with realistic number density (~SN rate × lifetime) and a `R(t)` law that _decelerates_.

## 2. Isolated/field SNe: runaways and Type Ia — holes with no local trigger

**SECONDARY** (search-synthesized from multiple review-level sources):

- **Runaway OB stars**: ~30–40% of O stars and ~5–10% of B stars are runaways; up to ~20–30% of the total OB population are field stars ejected from clusters (dynamical ejection or binary-supernova scenarios). Ejection velocities reach up to ~200 km/s (dynamical) or lower for the binary-SN channel. Over a massive-star lifetime of a few to tens of Myr, this **displaces the eventual SN site by hundreds of parsecs** from the birth cluster — far outside any coherent SF-event footprint.
- **Type Ia delay-time distribution**: **LITERATURE, verified (via search summary of specific findings, not full-text read)** — SNe Ia in the "star-formation deserts" (interarm-like regions) of spiral hosts have delay times truncated on the young side at **≳2 Gyr**, i.e., these are explosions of an old stellar population with no relation whatsoever to any recent or nearby star-formation event. More broadly, the accepted DTD for SNe Ia spans a "prompt" component (~40 Myr–1 Gyr) plus a long tail extending past several Gyr, roughly following a ~t⁻¹ power law (**RECALLED**, standard Maoz & Mannucci result, not independently verified this session).
- **Empirical evidence for SN-driven holes with no visible source (LITERATURE, verified — fetched arxiv abstract text directly)**: Rhode et al. (1999), testing the standard SN-driven hypothesis for HI holes in Holmberg II, found that in **86% of the 51 catalogued HI holes**, no remnant stellar cluster is visible; deep imaging showed several holes have limiting magnitudes fainter than what the putative SN-cluster hypothesis predicts, and many holes sit in **regions of very low optical surface brightness with no sign of recent star formation at all**. Only 3 of 51 holes even had associated FUV emission. Even in the Magellanic Clouds, where the massive-star/hole correlation is strongest, ~10% of holes still show no such link. The authors floated alternative origins (GRBs, high-velocity-cloud impacts, fountain return flows) precisely because the "hole ⇒ nearby young cluster" mapping fails empirically most of the time. [Caveat: Ho II is a dwarf, not an M74 analogue — the fraction should not be carried over numerically, but the existence of a large trigger-less hole population is the point.]

**Assessment**: This is the sharpest structural gap. The model's bubble-carving machinery is entirely **coupled to the discrete SF-event catalog** — bubbles only exist where a cataloged SF event exists, and only on arm ridges. But the empirical literature says a large fraction of real cavities have **no detectable local stellar trigger at all** — relics of runaways displaced hundreds of pc from their birth site, or of Gyr-old Type Ia progenitors sitting in the interarm/old-disk population. Capturing this needs a **decoupled cavity-seeding process**: cavities placed independent of the SF-event catalog's spatial footprint (including deep interarm, far from any arm ridge), driven by a runaway-displacement kernel and a separate old-population Ia rate, not by `armForcing`/`armFactor` at all. This is very likely the single largest reason the model's current interarm regions look empty of cavities while JWST/MIRI shows dark holes everywhere including deep interarm.

## 3. Dust destruction by SN shocks — not just displacement, actual grain loss

**LITERATURE, verified (via search-synthesized abstract text of Slavin, Jones & Dwek 2015 and follow-ups)**:

- Grain destruction in SN shocks proceeds via thermal sputtering, non-thermal/inertial sputtering, and shattering/vaporization from grain-grain collisions; efficiency is most divergent from steady-shock approximations at shock velocities **>200 km/s**, i.e., in the young/adiabatic phase of the remnant, which is exactly when a remnant is small and cavity-like.
- Silicate grain destruction timescales in the diffuse ISM, revised upward from Jones et al. (1996) after accounting for a warm-phase-dominated medium and updated SN rates/ISM mass, land around **~2–3 Gyr** — over cosmic time SNe are the dominant dust-destruction channel for the whole ISM, even though any single remnant only processes a small local patch.
- **PAH-specific destruction (SECONDARY, Micelotta et al. 2010 as reported by search)**: PAHs with ~50 carbon atoms do not survive shocks faster than **~100 km/s**; larger PAHs (~200 C atoms) survive to **~125 km/s**. This threshold sits squarely inside the velocity range of a young-to-mid-age SNR shock, meaning **PAH destruction is essentially guaranteed inside and immediately around any single-SN remnant while it is dynamically young** — this is directly the emission carrier that makes MIRI/PHANGS images bright, so its local destruction is what actually darkens a cavity in mid-IR, not gas/dust being geometrically pushed aside.
- Later work (Kirchschlager et al. 2022, reported via search) suggests the Slavin/Jones-style models may _overestimate_ shattering efficiency relative to observed shocked-grain populations in real SNRs, so treat exact destruction fractions as uncertain, but the qualitative point (SN shocks destroy grains locally, not just displace them) is not in dispute across the literature surveyed.

**Assessment**: This is a _mechanism-of-appearance_ gap, not just a missing-population gap. The model's dust bubbles are carved by **deleting dust density inside a growing sphere** — a purely geometric sweep. The literature says the real process is exactly the opposite emphasis: **destruction of the PAH carrier by shock heating/sputtering/shattering at velocities the model never represents**, not displacement. As-is, the model probably reproduces the qualitative _look_ of a dark cavity (dust density → 0 inside a sphere) but for the wrong reason — no shock velocity, no destruction physics, no dependence on whether the shock is still fast enough to matter. Since the visual endpoint (locally dust-free interior) is similar whether by destruction or geometric deletion, this gap mostly matters for physical credibility and for correctly timing _when_ a cavity should appear dark (immediately, while the shock is still fast) versus fade in gradually. One consequence that IS visual: destruction licenses cavity floors darker than any mass-conservation argument alone allows (see the image-morphology doc §8).

## 4. Clustered SNe: superbubbles keep growing after the ionizing glow is gone — and the model's R(t) has the wrong curvature

**LITERATURE, verified (search-synthesized quotes from the Weaver et al. 1977 framework and modern reviews)**:

- The classical energy-driven bubble solution (Weaver et al. 1977, extended by McCray & Kafatos 1987, Mac Low & McCray 1988) gives **`R_b(t) = 0.76 (L/ρ)^(1/5) t^(3/5)`** — a **decelerating** power law, with expansion velocity `v ∝ t^(-2/5)` falling monotonically.
- Stellar winds dominate energy input only up to **t ~ 5 Myr**; supernovae then continue injecting energy into the same cavity until **t ~ 5×10⁷ yr** (50 Myr), because for a coeval cluster the IMF slope nearly cancels the mass–lifetime relation, keeping the CCSN rate roughly constant for ~40 Myr after the first explosion. Crucially, **the same `t^(3/5)` growth law continues to apply through this transition** — the bubble keeps growing under sustained SN energy input long after the last O star has died and the HII glow has switched off. (The model's age axis does encode "old = big + no glow" — this part it gets right.)
- Caveats found in follow-up work: real superbubbles are systematically **smaller and X-ray-fainter than the idealized energy-conserving Weaver solution** predicts, because energy leaks to radiative losses and turbulent motions — but the _qualitative_ shape (continued, decelerating growth well past the ionizing-star lifetime) still holds.

**Direct comparison to the model's `age^2.5` law**: `age^2.5` is **convex/accelerating**; the real physics (Sedov-Taylor `t^0.4`, radiative `t^0.3`, superbubble `t^0.6`) is **uniformly decelerating** at every stage. As an R(t) claim this is a categorical mismatch.

**ORCHESTRATOR CORRECTION to the "just swap the exponent" fix.** The exponent does not exist to be an R(t) law — `dustBubblePlacements.ts`'s own comment says it approximates the **−2.2 size distribution** from the catalog's UNIFORM age draws ("many-small/few-big"). Under uniform ages, `R ∝ a^p` gives `dN/dR ∝ R^(1/p − 1)`: p=2.5 yields `R^-0.6` (many small, few big — qualitatively right, quantitatively shallower than −2.2), while the physically-correct p=0.6 yields `dN/dR ∝ R^+0.67` — an **inverted** distribution, mostly-big bubbles. Swapping the exponent alone would trade a wrong growth law for a wrong population. The coherent fix, if the age→size fiction should become physical: draw ages non-uniformly (many young) OR draw radii directly from the −2.2 power law and derive the age label from the decelerating R(t) inverse — either way, distribution and growth law must be co-designed, they cannot share one exponent.

**Assessment**: The gap lives in one mapping in the SF-event catalog, but per the correction above it is a two-constraint problem (size distribution AND growth curvature), not a one-line change. For a static snapshot renderer only the distribution is visible, so the current law's real defect is being ~1.6 dex shallower than −2.2, not its curvature; curvature starts to matter the moment anything animates or the age axis drives other correlated features (glow fade vs size).

## 5. SN-driven turbulence: velocity dispersion, fractal ISM texture, vertical venting

**LITERATURE, verified (search-synthesized quotes from Dib et al. 2006, Gatto et al. 2015, and a 2026 A&A comparison paper)**:

- Observed HI velocity dispersion in spiral disks: **σ ≈ 10–11 km/s** (warm neutral medium), fairly uniform across galaxy types.
- Pure SN-driven turbulence simulations systematically **underpredict** this: Dib et al. (2006) get σ ≈ 3–6 km/s for HI depending on SN rate (saturating, largely _independent_ of SN rate above a threshold); a 2026 study comparing directly to solar-neighborhood HI finds simulated σ = 4.9–6.7 km/s against observed 11.1 km/s. The shortfall is roughly a **factor of ~2**, persistent across two decades of independent simulation codes. Proposed resolutions: higher SN feedback efficiency, large-scale drivers (rotation/shear), thermal instability, disk-flaring effects.
- **Galactic fountains / chimneys (SECONDARY)**: superbubbles that grow past the gas scale height (typically **blowout at ~1–3 scale heights**, chimney widths ~100–150 pc in simulations) undergo Rayleigh–Taylor breakup and vent hot gas vertically, feeding a fountain flow recycling over kpc scales and multi-100-Myr timescales — a genuinely 3D, vertical-structure-dependent process.

**Assessment**: Explicitly out of scope for the current model — no velocity field, no turbulence concept, no third dimension for bubble dynamics. None of this can be captured with a small patch. The pragmatic fallback, if the goal is only _visual_ fidelity to the filamentary texture, is fractal/multi-octave noise calibrated against observed ISM density power spectra — capture the look, explicitly not the mechanism (which is what the detail-splat tier already intends).

## 6. The SSPSF lineage: what "spread" was meant to be, and what modern sims say about it

**LITERATURE, verified (search-synthesized, quotes drawn from multiple secondary review sources plus the Kim et al. 2020 TIGRESS abstract)**:

- **Mueller & Arnett (1976)** proposed the original stochastic self-propagating star-formation idea; **Gerola & Seiden (1978)** generalized it into the cellular-automaton form, with a star-forming cell's "supernova" triggering star formation in adjacent cells via **SN/stellar-wind shock compression raising local pressure above the threshold for the warm→cold gas phase transition**. This is _exactly_ the physical process skymap's CA `spread` term stands in for — Gerola & Seiden's own stated mechanism is shock-triggered collapse.
- **Modern verdict from TIGRESS (Kim et al. 2020, ApJ 898, 35; LITERATURE, verified via search-synthesized abstract text)**: in fully hydrodynamic, SN-feedback-resolving local-box simulations of a spiral potential, **>90% of star formation does occur in spiral arms**, but the _global_ SFR enhancement from having arms at all is **less than a factor of 2** over the no-arm control. The paper's explicit interpretation: **"spiral arms do not trigger star formation but rather concentrate star-forming regions"** — the arm potential's gravitational focusing of gas, not SN-shock triggering, does the structuring work. Correlated SN feedback does produce **arm spurs/feathers downstream of arms**, but short-lived ones (distinct from the longer-lived gravitationally-induced feathers).
- **SILCC (Gatto/Girichidis/Walch et al., search-synthesized, SECONDARY)**: molecular clouds form preferentially at the shells/intersections of SN-driven bubbles in these simulations, consistent with _some_ shell-triggered collapse — but with the explicit literature caveat that **"this mechanism is not generally confirmed by observations."**

**Assessment**: The most important framing result for judging the CA design. The lineage confirms the `spread` term is a faithful abstraction of what Gerola & Seiden intended. But the modern, physically-resolved verdict is that SN-triggered propagation was never the dominant structuring agent: real spiral structure comes overwhelmingly from gravitational gas concentration, with propagation contributing <2× local enhancement and short-lived downstream spurs. Note this **agrees with the branch's own architecture decision** — arms enter as given forcing and the CA only generates the detail hanging off them (sf-map.md: "SSPSF explicitly does NOT produce grand design"). The residual caveat is subtler: within the detail tier itself, `armForcing` conflates gravitational concentration and SN triggering into one term, and the literature says the two have different spatial signatures (concentration: symmetric about the ridge; triggering: downstream-biased spurs). If the survey-to-params map ever wants those to vary independently, they are two knobs, not one.
