# Cloud-Forming Regions and Observed Dust Structure: Literature Review

Context: comparing skymap's SSPSF cellular-automaton dust model (activity map IS the dust density; detail splats add sub-grid texture; ~125 discrete SF events on arm ridges) against the physical chain that actually produces a JWST MIRI PAH image like NGC 628's.

---

## 1. GMC formation mechanisms: instability, spacing, spurs/feathers

**LITERATURE, verified** (arxiv abstract fetch + search-result excerpts, Dobbs review): GMC formation theories fall into a small number of mechanisms, all reducible to converging flows (negative velocity divergence): (a) top-down gravitational/Toomre instability of the sheared, self-gravitating gas disc, which can build clouds up to ≳10⁷ M☉ directly; (b) bottom-up colliding/converging flows (turbulence-, supernova-, or spiral-shock-driven), which tend to produce smaller clouds; (c) magneto-gravitational (Parker) instability. Parker instability alone is now disfavored as the dominant GMC-formation channel: its fastest-growing mode for a straight field is essentially scale-free (favoring many small condensations, not GMC-sized ones); under uniform gravity its growth time is ~1.2×10⁸ yr at a ~1.6 kpc wavelength — both too slow and too large compared to observed cloud lifetimes (~3×10⁷ yr) and mean cloud separation (~0.5 kpc); and its density enhancement factor is only ~3×, far short of the ~10× needed. A skewed/disturbed field geometry or nonlinear triggering by a spiral shock can rescue it as a secondary contributor. **SECONDARY**: mass-segregated hybrid picture — Parker instability seeds lower-mass clouds (~10⁵ M☉) where self-gravity is weak; gravitational/magneto-Jeans instability dominates for the massive (~10⁶–10⁷ M☉) complexes, largely independent of magnetic support.

**LITERATURE, verified** (La Vigne et al. 2006 summarized via search, cross-checked against multiple follow-ups): the resulting beads-on-a-string / spur-feather pattern in grand-design spiral dust lanes has quasi-regular spacing **300–800 pc**, increasing with galactocentric radius as local gas surface density falls (spacing anti-correlates with Σ_gas, consistent with a Jeans-length origin: spacing ≈ 5–10 local Jeans lengths in the arm dust lane). Follow-up "wiggle instability" theoretical work (2020s) reproduces a broader range, 200–2000 pc, and ties the spacing to the linear gravitational instability of the arm gas ridge itself — i.e., the arm is unstable to fragmentation on the Jeans scale set by its own compressed density, not merely a passive host for pre-existing clumps. Feathers (dust) and spurs (young stars) are the same underlying gravitational fragmentation viewed in absorption vs. emission; feathers occur preferentially in strong-spiral-potential (grand-design, Sc/Sb) galaxies with prominent shocks, and weaken in flocculent (Sc/Sd, weak-potential) discs. CO follow-up in M51 confirms feather = molecular-gas spur (excellent positional coincidence). Feather column density in CO falls by a factor of 5–10 within a few hundred pc of the arm/dust-lane, pinpointing the arm as the fragmentation site, with shear stretching material into the interarm as it flows out.

**Assessment.** Skymap's arm forcing texture is a static multiplier on ignition probability; it has no mechanism that converts the compressed arm gas ridge into a _quantized_, Jeans-spaced set of fragmentation sites — ignition is a smooth stochastic field, not a discretized bead pattern. The **discrete SF-event catalog (~125 events) is the natural home for this**: today those events are placed with uniform random ages/positions along arm ridges with no spacing model. Minimal machinery to capture the "beads on a string" look: replace uniform placement with a 1D Poisson-disc / quasi-periodic sampling along each arm ridge polyline at a radius-dependent spacing (anchor: 300–800 pc, growing outward), independent of the CA's ignition probability. This is a placement-rule change, not a new physics module — cheap, and it's the single highest-leverage fix for "the image doesn't look like beads on a string" because it's the only model piece with the right granularity (arm-ridge, not cell-scale) to carry a 300–800 pc periodicity. The CA itself, at ~tens-of-pc cells over log-polar 768×256, is coarser than a single feather spacing by only ~3–10× — marginal but not hopeless for a _smoothed_ spur/feather envelope; it cannot resolve the underlying Jeans fragmentation (that would need cell sizes well under 100 pc and an actual local self-gravity/dispersion-relation term, which the CA does not have).

## 2. GMC lifecycle and the PHANGS "uncertainty principle" decorrelation

**LITERATURE, verified** (Chevance et al. 2020, arxiv:1911.03479 abstract fetched directly): applying the Kruijssen–Longmore statistical "uncertainty principle for star formation" method (aperture-based CO-to-Hα flux ratio vs. aperture size, no cloud catalog needed) to 9 nearby PHANGS-ALMA discs:

- **GMC lifetime: 10–30 Myr**, varying with environment (both between and within galaxies).
- Environmental split at Σ_H2 = 8 M☉ pc⁻²: above this, cloud lifetime tracks galactic dynamical timescales (shear, large-scale gravitational collapse); below it, cloud lifetime is set by internal dynamics (free-fall/crossing time), decoupled from galactic-scale dynamics.
- **Long inert phase**: 75–90% of the cloud lifetime passes with no massive star formation.
- **Fast dispersal**: once massive stars turn on, the cloud is dispersed within **1–5 Myr**.
- **Mean CO–HII separation length: 100–300 pc** in star-forming discs — the _spatial_ signature of the same decorrelation (clouds and the HII regions they will eventually spawn are offset, not cospatial, because of the time lag plus galactic shear during the inert phase).
- Integrated star-formation efficiency per cloud: 4–10%.

**SECONDARY** (search-result synthesis of the companion feedback-timescale paper, Chevance/Kim et al. follow-ups): a separately-measured "feedback timescale" (time from first Hα/24 μm detection to cloud dispersal) of 2–7 Myr, comprising 17–47% of the total cloud lifetime, is shorter than the time to the first core-collapse supernova (4–20 Myr) — implying **pre-supernova feedback (photoionization, stellar winds, radiation pressure) is what actually disperses the cloud**, not SNe. (Numbers from this companion paper and the headline Chevance 2020 abstract differ slightly in how they carve up the "embedded"/"feedback" phases — both are consistent in showing the destructive phase is short, ≲5–7 Myr, versus a long ≥20 Myr quiescent phase.)

**Why this matters for a model where "SF map IS dust map":** the empirical finding is that molecular gas (proto-dust reservoir) and recently-formed massive stars/HII regions are _anti-correlated on cloud scales_ — you see one or the other, offset by ~100–300 pc and by the 10–30 Myr lifecycle, not both cospatial. A model that paints dust density directly from "activity" (which spikes at/after ignition, i.e., near young massive stars) is painting dust where the _stars_ are, which is close to the opposite of where the gas reservoir dominates for most of the cycle: during the 75–90%-of-lifetime inert phase there is a dense gas/dust cloud with _no_ activity signal at all, and after ignition the activity signal appears exactly where the gas is now being destroyed and dispersed within 1–5 Myr.

**Assessment.** This is the deepest physics gap and it lives squarely in the **CA SF-map-as-dust identification**, not in the detail splats or the event catalog. As-is, the model cannot produce the PHANGS decorrelation because it has collapsed two anti-correlated fields (gas/dust reservoir vs. massive-star activity) into one channel. Minimal machinery to capture it without a full gas-dynamics solve: give the CA a _second_, longer-lived field — call it "reservoir" or "gas-not-yet-consumed" — that is high before ignition and decays only over the 10–30 Myr lifecycle (not the instantaneous activity EMA), and let _that_ field (not raw activity) drive dust density; activity/age would instead drive a _local suppression_ kernel (a "hole," ~1–5 Myr FWHM, offset toward younger ignited cells) subtracted from the dust map near recent ignitions, and a small positional offset (~100–300 pc, shear-sheared) between the ignition marker and the dust deficit it causes. That is a second scalar CA channel plus a decay/suppression rule, not a new subsystem — but it is a real addition, not a knob tune, because the current single-field design has no state that persists once activity fades (the "long inert phase" has no representation at all). [Orchestrator note: the CA does already carry a `gas` channel — see the synthesis doc for how far that existing channel gets toward this fix.]

## 3. Dust/gas density structure inside and between clouds

**LITERATURE, verified** (search-synthesized from Kainulainen and the lognormal/power-law transition literature; consistent across many independent groups): column-density PDFs (N-PDFs) of molecular clouds are lognormal in _quiescent_, non-star-forming clouds (supersonic turbulence alone), but star-forming clouds develop an added **power-law tail at high column density**, attributed to the onset of self-gravity/collapse. This lognormal-core + power-law-tail shape, and its evolution from purely lognormal (early, turbulence-dominated) to power-law-tailed (late, gravity-dominated) as a cloud evolves toward star formation, is now a standard result across simulations and Herschel-based observations (dust continuum and extinction maps agree). Some clouds show a _second_, shallower power law at the very highest columns (dense cores), i.e., a broken power-law tail rather than a single slope — reported only in the densest massive-GMC regions.

**LITERATURE, verified for the finding, but the "universal 0.1 pc" claim itself is contested** (Arzoumanian et al. 2011/2019 vs. Panopoulou et al. critique, both via search): Herschel Gould Belt filaments show a median deconvolved FWHM width of **~0.1 pc (0.10 ± 0.03 pc)**, proposed as a characteristic/universal scale tied to the sonic length where turbulence goes subsonic. This has been _reproduced_ by several independent studies (Salji et al. 2015: 0.08 pc; Koch & Rosolowsky 2015; Shimajiri et al. 2023) even for filaments with very high line mass. But a direct challenge (Panopoulou et al., A&A) shows per-cloud mean filament width scales with distance ∝ beam size (~4–5 beams), and concludes the "universal 0.1 pc" is largely a **resolution artifact**, not a physical constant. Net: the _existence_ of a narrow, sub-pc characteristic filament width in real GMCs is well supported; its exact universal value is not settled, but 0.1 pc is the right order of magnitude to cite as "far below anything a galaxy-scale grid can resolve."

**Assessment.** These are structures at 0.03–1 pc (filaments, cores, the lognormal-to-power-law PDF transition) inside a CA cell that is itself tens of pc. The skymap model's detail splats (super-Gaussian lane edges, fractal along-lane modulation, negative bubble splats) are explicitly _sub-grid_ stand-ins for this — and that is architecturally the right call; no CA at 768×256 log-polar will ever resolve a 0.1 pc filament directly. The gap is that the current splats are a generic fractal/super-Gaussian vocabulary, not shaped by the actual PDF result: real dust column has a lognormal _bulk_ (broad, moderate-contrast fractal texture — plausibly what the fractal modulation already gives) plus a **distinct, rarer, much higher-contrast power-law tail** concentrated at filament ridgelines and cores (central A_V~10 anchors this — already a literature anchor in the current design). Minimal machinery: sample splat peak amplitudes from a two-population mixture (broad lognormal-like jitter + a sparse power-law-tailed set of bright ridge/core splats) rather than one continuous fractal field, so that "most of the area is modestly structured, a small area is very opaque" — which is what actually distinguishes a GMC's N-PDF from generic Perlin-like fractal noise. This is a tuning/statistics change to the existing splat generator, not a new subsystem.

## 4. Arm vs. interarm contrast

**LITERATURE, verified** (PHANGS-ALMA CO(2–1), search-synthesized from the IOPscience "Organization of Cloud-scale Gas Density Structure" paper and Sun et al. 2020/2022 GMC-catalog results, cross-checked against an independent ALMA-LEGUS study): the picture is one of **overlap with modest contrast, not stark dichotomy**. Cloud-scale (150 pc) CO surface-brightness contrasts are 3–4× larger than matched 3.6 μm (stellar) contrasts, and spiral-arm/interarm CO contrast tracks the Querejeta et al. morphological arm definition reasonably well. At the GMC-catalog level (Sun et al. 2020, Rosolowsky et al. 2021): arm and interarm cloud populations **largely overlap** in property space; arm clouds have a truncation mass ~2.5× higher than interarm, modestly higher surface densities, and slightly lower virial parameters (1.2 vs. 1.4, i.e., somewhat more bound) — but these are factor-of-1.5–2.5 differences, not order-of-magnitude ones. A companion PHANGS-JWST PAH-cloud catalog finds a similar 1.5–2.5× arm/interarm molecular-surface-density ratio. One independent LEGUS-ALMA study reports even _less_ arm/interarm surface-density difference than Sun et al., calling their own concordance with the literature "surprising." Feather/spur CO column falls 5–10× within a few hundred pc of the arm (§1) — so _within_ the interarm, structure is strongly non-uniform (bright near-arm feathers fading outward), which is a different statement from a flat "interarm is N× fainter than arm."

**RECALLED** (not verified this session, flagged accordingly): the standard picture that at least _some_ interarm structure is genuinely locally-formed (via converging flows/Toomre instability off-arm, not exclusively sheared arm debris) rather than 100% arm-remnant feathers — PHANGS interarm-cloud papers report interarm clouds with independent kinematics inconsistent with pure passive shearing of arm-formed material in some galaxies. Treat this specific claim as unverified pending a direct fetch.

**Assessment.** This is good news for the CA design's core premise (arms as forcing texture, but ignition still stochastic everywhere) — a factor of ~2–3 arm/interarm dust-surface-density ratio, with real texture in both regions, is exactly the kind of thing a smooth multiplicative `armFactor` on ignition probability _can_ produce, given the right calibration. The place the current design likely under- or over-shoots is the near-arm feather gradient (5–10× falloff over a few hundred pc, §1) — a sharp near-field structure the static arm-forcing texture is unlikely to reproduce without an explicit distance-from-ridge falloff term. Minimal machinery: shape `armForcing`'s spatial profile with a steep near-ridge decay (not just azimuthal envelope) so the model's own interarm gradient matches the observed 5–10×/few-hundred-pc falloff, rather than relying on the CA's stochastic spread term to produce it emergently (which it likely cannot, being purely local/diffusive with no directional shear-stretch term).

## 5. What MIRI PAH emission actually traces

**LITERATURE, verified** (search-synthesized from Leroy et al. 2023, Chastenet et al. 2023a/b, Egorov/Sandstrom et al. 2023, all PHANGS-JWST First Results, cross-checked across independent search hits): MIRI F770W and F1130W are PAH-feature-dominated (narrow filters centered on the 7.7/11.3 μm PAH bands; minimal hot-dust-continuum contamination except toward mid-IR-bright point sources or the galaxy nucleus). These bands are the _preferred_ high-resolution proxies for gas/dust column — **preferred over F2100W (21 μm)**, which instead traces hot small-grain continuum tied more directly to the local radiation field / total IR luminosity, not column density per se. Resolution at NGC 628's distance: MIRI ≈ 12–32 pc per beam depending on band — genuinely cloud/filament-scale.

Critically, **PAH abundance itself is not constant**: PAH fraction (qPAH) is strongly **anti-correlated with the ionization parameter of HII regions** (Egorov/Sandstrom, ~1500 HII regions across 4 galaxies) — PAHs are destroyed by hard/intense radiation fields inside and near HII regions, an effect that steepens for more luminous HII regions and only weakly depends on metallicity or region age (Hα equivalent width). Near HII regions, surviving PAHs also skew smaller/more ionized (harder radiation field selects smaller grain sizes, via F335M/F1130W and F335M/F770W ratios). Net: **MIRI PAH brightness = f(PAH abundance) × (column) × (radiation-field heating)**, and PAH abundance itself drops right where the radiation field is strongest — a partially self-limiting, non-monotonic relationship between "more massive stars nearby" and "brighter PAH emission," not a simple product of two independent factors.

Diffuse-ISM behavior (Sandstrom et al. 2023, "Tracing the Diffuse ISM..."): below ~10 M☉ pc⁻², the PAH-traced gas distribution in NGC 628/1365/7496 shows a **"Swiss-cheese" topology** — holes and bubbles pervading an otherwise smooth diffuse component — a signature directly attributable to feedback carving cavities, not simply structure "not yet formed."

Dust filament network specifically for NGC 628 (Thilker et al. 2023, arxiv:2301.00881, fetched directly): filaments identified at multiple scales, characteristic width ≈25 pc at the finest resolvable scale, up to ~400 pc for the largest coherent structures; **75–80% of HII regions** lie within the filament network; **60% of star clusters younger than 5 Myr** lie within filaments; **~30% of total MIR flux** originates in the filament network; morphological agreement between MIR filaments and optical extinction lanes in ~40% of sightlines (i.e., a large minority of dust structure is _not_ simultaneously visible in both tracers — consistent with the emission/extinction asymmetry in §6). An anti-correlation was found between the filament network and _looser_ (unbound-looking) NUV-selected stellar associations <5 Myr — i.e., once clusters disperse from their natal filament (or destroy it), the correlation breaks.

**Assessment.** This is the section with the largest gap relative to the model's current physics, and it lives in the **detail-splat / dust-rendering layer**, not the CA. Skymap's dust rendering is a pure absorption screen driven by density; it has no emission physics and no radiation-field dependence, so it cannot reproduce the qualitative MIRI look where brightness peaks are set by illumination geometry (proximity to young massive stars) as much as by column, and where PAH abundance is actually _suppressed_ in the brightest, most irradiated interiors (giving the characteristic bright-rim/dark-cavity structure around HII regions, not simply bright=dense). Minimal machinery to get closer without building a real radiative-transfer pass: treat the rendered "MIRI-like" brightness as density × a heating proxy (distance-weighted flux from nearby ignited/high-activity cells, already available from the CA's activity field) with a saturating or turnover term so that very close to strong sources the PAH term is suppressed (bright rim, then a dark, PAH-depleted interior) rather than monotonically brighter — i.e., a shell/rim shader term keyed off distance-to-nearest-recent-ignition, layered on top of the existing absorption screen as an _emission_ add rather than folded into density. This does not require abandoning "dust density from activity map" for the extinction pass — it requires adding a second, emission-side term for any MIRI-style visualization, which the current single absorption-screen architecture doesn't have a slot for at all.

## 6. Extinction vs. emission: same structures, inverted (and non-identical) contrast

**SECONDARY/RECALLED mix, general astrophysics established but not freshly verified against a single authoritative source this session**: the basic physical statement — dust grains absorbing starlight in the optical/UV heat up and re-radiate thermally in the IR/PAH bands, so the same physical dust structures appear dark against a bright stellar background in optical extinction maps and bright against a faint background in IR emission maps — is well-established textbook physics (search results turned up multiple consistent examples: M51 8 μm vs. Hα/3.6 μm, NGC 253, NGC 1482, Milky Way infrared dark clouds). This part is safe as **RECALLED-but-uncontroversial**.

What is _not_ a trivial palette inversion, and **is** directly literature-grounded from §5 above (Thilker et al. 2023, verified): only ~40% of sightlines show detailed morphological agreement between MIR emission filaments and optical extinction lanes. The two tracers diverge because (a) extinction requires a background light source behind/through the dust (geometry-dependent — a dense cloud with nothing bright behind it is invisible in extinction but can still be a strong PAH emitter if illuminated from the side/front, and vice versa), and (b) PAH emission additionally requires _radiation-field heating and surviving PAH abundance_ (§5) — so a cold, quiescent, gravity-dominated dense clump deep in a cloud's power-law tail (§3) can be a strong extinction feature and a weak-to-absent PAH emitter, while a lower-column but freshly-irradiated filament edge can be extinction-invisible but PAH-bright.

**Assessment.** Skymap's renderer only implements the extinction/absorption half of this pair (dust as a multiplicative absorption screen against background starlight). That is actually the _simpler_ and more self-consistent half to have chosen, since it requires no emission/heating model — a defensible scope cut, not an error. But it means any visual comparison to a MIRI/PAH reference image (like NGC 628's) is comparing an extinction renderer's output to an emission-tracer photograph — two representations of the same underlying dust field that the literature says agree only ~40% of the time on fine structure, and diverge systematically in exactly the regions this renderer's SF-map-as-dust identification cares about most (near recent/active star formation, where PAH is destroyed but column is often still high). If the rendering goal is genuinely "look like the MIRI image," the absorption-screen architecture is the wrong tool for that specific target and needs the emission-term addition sketched in §5; if the goal is "plausible dust lanes as seen against starlight" (a more classical optical/near-IR look, e.g. HST dark-lane comparisons), the current absorption-only architecture is already the physically appropriate choice and the MIRI image should be used only as a _structural_ reference (filament network topology, bead spacing, bubble/cavity presence) rather than a brightness/contrast reference.
