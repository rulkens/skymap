## 1. Weaver et al. 1977 wind-blown bubble theory: phases, scaling, drivers

**SECONDARY** (aggregated from citing papers, Weaver 1977 abstract not directly fetched). The classic model (Castor, McCray & Weaver 1975; Weaver, McCray, Castor, Shapiro & Moore 1977, _ApJ_ 218, 377) partitions a wind/SNe-blown cavity into four zones: (1) freely expanding wind, (2) shocked wind (hot, low-density, high-pressure — occupies most of the volume), (3) a thin, dense shocked-ambient shell bounded by a contact discontinuity and an outer radiative shock, (4) undisturbed ambient medium. Two dynamical regimes:

- **Energy-conserving (adiabatic) phase**: interior retains its thermal energy, drives the shell via pdV work. `R_ED(t) = (125/154π · L_w t³ / ρ̄)^(1/5)` → **R ∝ t^(3/5)**.
- **Momentum-conserving phase**: once the interior radiates its energy away (or leaks it), the shell coasts on accumulated momentum → **R ∝ t^(1/2)**, slower growth.
- A recent (post-2020) reassessment (**RECALLED**, not independently verified here) argues single/small-cluster wind bubbles are _not_ well described by the energy-conserving solution and are closer to momentum-driven throughout — relevant because skymap's ~125 discrete events are individual-region-scale, not always large OB associations.

**LITERATURE, verified** — Barnes et al. 2023 (the "Phantom Void," NGC 628) apply the model directly and get real numbers: for a ~500 pc bubble with mean stellar age ~10 Myr, `v_exp ≃ 0.6R/t = 30 km/s`, matching independent kinematic measurements (molecular out-of-plane ~16 km/s, ionized gas ~45 km/s, adopted range 15–50 km/s). Dynamical age ≈ **10 Myr** for a **~500–800 pc** structure. Energetics: shell mass 4.4×10⁷ M☉ (H₂+HI combined), kinetic energy 1.7×10⁵³ erg at v_exp~20 km/s → requires **~100 supernovae**, i.e. this is not a single-star bubble but a sustained cluster/association output. The authors explicitly invoke Mac Low & McCray 1988: continuous energy injection (many sequential SNe) is needed to keep interior pressure — and hence v_exp — high; a single SN's pressure would already have dropped by this radius.

**Assessment**: The model's discrete-event catalog assigns each event a strength and a bubble radius from `age^2.5` scaling calibrated to Watkins' −2.2 size-power-law, which is a reasonable phenomenological stand-in for _some_ R(t) relation, but it is not Weaver's R∝t^0.6 — the exponent is baked in as a shape-matching choice, not derived from an energy/momentum budget, and there's no explicit expansion-velocity field or interior-pressure state. This is fine for a static-frame "one CPU catalog, one time snapshot" renderer — **captures the look for the right general reason (self-similar growth, larger=older) but the wrong mechanism**; no new machinery is needed unless a future feature wants time-evolving/animated bubbles, at which point true R(t) with an explicit ED→MD transition (and cooling-radius transition, see §2) would need adding to the event catalog as a small ODE or lookup table.

## 2. Shell sweep-up: why cavity walls carry the PAH filaments

**LITERATURE, verified** (Barnes et al. 2023). Swept-up material is measurably concentrated in the shell versus the interior: shell H₂ mass 3.8×10⁷ M☉ vs. interior 1.3×10⁶ M☉; shell HI 5.6×10⁶ M☉ vs. interior 7.0×10⁵ M☉ — roughly a **30–8× mass enhancement in the shell relative to the cleared cavity**. Column densities: Σ_H₂(shell) = 22.8 M☉/pc², Σ_HI(shell) = 3.4 M☉/pc². The paper states directly that MIRI F770W dark/bright contrast is a density effect: "increased gas (and therefore dust) column densities in swept-up shells" make bubble features stand out.

**LITERATURE, verified** (Thilker et al. 2023, dust filament network). At 25 pc filament width, dust filaments cover ~50–55% of disc sightlines, rising to ~60–65% at 200 pc scale — i.e. the filamentary network, not the cavities, is the majority-area structure once you're not restricting to the brightest ridges. Filaments at 25 pc scale are long (l > 1 kpc) and high-aspect-ratio — consistent with them being shared shell walls rather than isolated compact clumps. One caveat worth flagging: Thilker et al. report that filament detection is concentrated "in spiral arms (markedly less so in interarm regions)" at the scales/thresholds they used — this is a _detection-significance_ statement (interarm PAH/dust surface brightness is fainter, so the algorithm's filament mask is sparser there), not necessarily a claim that interarm cavities/walls don't exist; it should be read as a caveat to the "foam is present everywhere at equal contrast" framing, not a refutation of it.

**Assessment**: This is the crux mechanism and it is where the model has a real, nameable gap. The physical picture is: dust is _swept and compressed into rims_, producing a coherent, connected network of bright walls, each wall shared by (typically) two adjacent bubbles. The model's negative-splat approach _deletes_ density inside a bubble footprint but does nothing constructive to the material at the boundary — there is no rim/wall enhancement, no mass conservation moving the deleted gas onto a shell. Two bubbles placed near each other in the model will each carve their own hole into the ambient dust screen; the region between them will look like unperturbed background, not a bright compressed wall. **This is a real missing-machinery gap, in the negative-splat piece specifically**: minimal fix is a companion _positive_ rim splat (a thin shell of enhanced density at the bubble boundary, radius-tied to the same event) — cheap to add since the event catalog already carries position + radius per bubble; get the mass budget from "swept volume × ambient density," not a real hydro solve. Without it, overlapping/adjacent holes in the CA-driven activity map plus catalog dust cavities will look like isolated dark spots on a flat field rather than a tessellated foam of bright walls around dark cells — exactly the failure mode the target image would expose immediately.

## 3. Triggered star formation on rims

**SECONDARY/RECALLED** (aggregated web summary, not a single fetched primary) for the general "collect-and-collapse" (Elmegreen & Lada 1977; Whitworth et al. 1994) mechanism: an expanding ionization-shock front sweeps a decelerating layer of neutral gas ahead of it; the layer becomes gravitationally unstable along its length and fragments once it crosses a critical surface density, forming new (often massive) clumps at the shell's _inner_ edge. Two competing fragmentation timescales in Whitworth's model — a short one (crossing-time instability, low-mass fragments seen ahead of the shell) and a long one (gravitational instability along the full shell, higher-mass fragments/clusters). Direct confirmation in individual Galactic HII regions remains contested in the literature (young stellar objects on rims are often embedded and hard to age-date cleanly).

**LITERATURE, verified** (Barnes et al. 2023, direct NGC 628 evidence). Ten young stellar associations with ages <5 Myr found within the Phantom Void's shell, masses up to 10⁵ M☉; total young (<20 Myr) stellar mass 10⁶·⁰ M☉, split **80% in the shell / 20% in the bubble interior**. Compact Hα peaks cluster around ~600 pc radius (near the shell) while NUV emission peaks closer in (~100 pc) — read as older clusters having "already evacuated their immediate environment." Crucially, HST-NUV young stars sit **preferentially on the _inner_ edge of the shell**, offset 50–100 pc from the 7.7 μm emission peak — i.e. stars sit just inside (cavity-ward of) the dust wall, not on top of it, matching the collect-and-collapse geometry (stars form in the swept gas, the emitting dust wall is slightly further out at the shock front). The authors offer three non-exclusive triggering scenarios: shell collapse of unbound swept gas, pre-existing bound clouds carried outward by the shell, or purely sequential star formation age-gradient within a single cloud — they don't discriminate between them.

**Assessment**: The 125-event catalog _already_ places young SF knots preferentially on arm ridges and gives each a strength/age, and dust cavities are carved for young events too — this captures the coarse correlation ("bright knots near dark holes") but for a different underlying reason than collect-and-collapse: in the model, a young HII knot and an old bubble are drawn **independently** — no correlation between a new SF event and an old bubble's rim. The literature mechanism requires the new star specifically to sit on the rim of an _older, causally-related_ structure. Reproducing the "knots on rims" look statistically (not causally) is a moderate lift: seed a fraction of new-event positions by sampling on existing bubble boundaries in the CA activity/catalog state rather than independently on the arm-ridge texture — this is new machinery in the **event catalog's placement logic**, not the CA and not the splat renderer. Purely visually, since the catalog already skews young events toward arm ridges where old bubbles also cluster, some coincidental rim-adjacency will appear "for free," but it will be statistically indistinguishable from noise rather than the tight rim-hugging PHANGS shows.

## 4. Bubble interactions: nesting, mergers, foam transition, size distribution

**LITERATURE, verified** (Watkins et al. 2023, direct fetch). Full catalog: **1,694 bubbles**, radii **6–552 pc**, mean/median 34/27 pc (16th–84th percentile 17–45 pc). Size distribution is a power law with index **p = −2.2 ± 0.1**, turnover at 29±3 pc — this is the number the model explicitly calibrates against, confirmed accurate. Theoretical comparison: models predict p = −2.7 for Milky Way-like pressure; from NGC 628's own Hα luminosity function slope the expected range is −2.4±0.2 to −2.7 — the observed distribution is **shallower than theory by 1–3.5σ** (more large bubbles than a simple non-interacting population would predict).

**Nesting**: "31% of bubbles overlap with at least one smaller bubble" at their boundary (using an annular mask at 0.9–1.1× radius) — this occurs **3.2× more often than random chance** would predict. The authors' own causal attribution is notable and cuts against a naive reading: they attribute the excess nesting primarily to **bubble merging** (smaller structures absorbed into a larger one's rim as it expands), not to triggered star formation, and state merging "best explains" the shallower-than-theory size slope. This is an important nuance for the model's design rationale — the 31% figure is real and directly reusable, but the paper's own explanation for _why_ it happens is closer to "the CA's neighboring-cell activity coupling" (structures interacting/merging over time) than to "one discrete event ignites next to another's rim."

**Assessment**: The size-distribution slope (−2.2) is already matched by construction, so that specific quantitative anchor is captured _by design_, not emergently — fine, since it's a calibration target, not a prediction. Nesting/merging is where the two model pieces diverge from the physical picture: the CA can plausibly produce merged/overlapping activity regions since neighbours interact stochastically (Gerola-Seiden ignition literally requires a lit neighbour), so the **CA piece has the right causal structure for merging** in the "SF map" layer. But the _discrete bubble catalog_ (~125 dust-cavity events, capped at 120 largest) draws ages/positions independently and has no merge/absorb step — two catalog bubbles can overlap in the negative-splat carve, which will look coincidentally "nested" in the density field, but nothing in the catalog logic represents one bubble growing into or consuming another. **No new machinery is strictly required to get a visually plausible nesting rate** (independent placement with realistic density already produces _some_ geometric overlap, and Watkins' own analysis shows real nesting is only 3.2× above chance, i.e. chance alone is already most of the signal) — but the transition to a _true space-filling foam_ (McKee & Ostriker 1977 three-phase ISM; Cox & Smith 1974 "hot tunnels" — **SECONDARY**, a supernova rate of ~1/50 yr is enough for remnants to overlap into a percolating hot network) needs the cavities to be dense enough, on average, to actually tile the interarm regions too, which caps at "120 largest" bubbles will likely undershoot for a whole-disc PAH-foam look; that's a coverage/count-budget tuning question rather than a new-mechanism question.

## 5. Shear elongation and blowout/chimneys

**LITERATURE, verified** (Watkins et al. 2023). Bubbles are measurably non-circular: median axis ratio q = 0.9, mean 0.8. Position-angle offset from the local spiral-arm tangent (Δθ) shows a non-uniform distribution peaking at Δθ = 0° and −30°, with a _deficit_ at +30° (Rayleigh test: 0.07% chance of a uniform distribution — a real, significant signal). Authors' interpretation: **differential shear stretches bubbles parallel to the arm**, and a secondary population near Δθ ≈ ±90° may reflect "blistering" into lower-density gas downstream of the arm (flagged by the authors themselves as tentative, needing further work). Separately, downstream-of-arm bubbles are systematically larger than upstream ones (mean 18%/median 9% larger; 99th-percentile radius 220 pc downstream vs 149 pc upstream, a 48% gap, Mann-Whitney p≈0.0001) — a differential-shear/arm-passage signature at the _population_ level, distinct from single-bubble shape elongation.

**LITERATURE, verified** (Barnes et al. 2023) for blowout: the Phantom Void (~800 pc shell) is visible even in low-resolution HI, and given NGC 628's HI disc scale height of ~800 pc (comparable to the bubble's own size), the authors conclude it has "likely burst out of the galaxy" — i.e. genuine vertical blowout into a chimney/fountain (Heiles 1984 chimneys; Fraternali 2017 fountains — **RECALLED** citations, not independently verified), though no direct hot-halo/X-ray evidence is presented (they flag Chandra follow-up as future work).

**Assessment**: This is squarely a 2D vs. 3D/kinematic gap. The model's SF map and bubble catalog are evaluated at the disc-plane crossing (2.5D), with no velocity field and no vertical/3D structure for bubbles at all. Shear elongation specifically is tractable cheaply: the CA already has differential shear Ω−Ω_pattern as a first-class term drifting the neighbour set, so an anisotropic (elongated-along-shear) kernel for the _discrete_ bubble catalog's negative splats — stretching each carved cavity along the local shear direction using the same Ω(r) already computed for the CA — would reproduce the axis-ratio/PA statistics essentially for free, reusing an existing quantity rather than adding new physics. Blowout/chimneys are a different story: reproducing "largest bubbles rupture vertically" requires the model to know something about vertical structure (a disc scale height) it currently doesn't model at all — this is a genuine "only with new machinery" gap, and it's probably not worth adding for a face-on/optical-thin-screen renderer unless a specific visual cue (e.g. a slightly different fade/transparency treatment for the largest cataloged bubbles, as a cheap proxy for "this one is punching through") is wanted.

## 6. PAH destruction inside HII regions and hot bubble interiors

**LITERATURE, verified** (Egorov et al. 2023, PHANGS-JWST + MUSE, ~1500 HII regions across 4 galaxies incl. NGC 628). Strong anti-correlation between PAH fraction (R_PAH) and HII-region ionization parameter, fit as `R_PAH = (−4.58±0.16)·log([SIII]/[SII]) + (1.99±0.06)`, steeper for more luminous regions. Authors' conclusion: **hydrogen-ionizing UV radiation is the dominant PAH-destruction mechanism** (ionization/sputtering of the PAH molecules within the ionized volume itself), not simply "hard" UV spectral shape. Metallicity is a minor secondary factor (weak within the ~0.3 dex range covered). PAH fraction also decreases with Hα equivalent width (an age proxy) but this trend is markedly weaker than the ionization-parameter trend. Depletion is spatially confined to the ionized-gas mask itself in this analysis (no explicit statement about how far into neutral gas the destruction extends) — **destruction tracks ionization state, not just "inside a cavity."**

**Assessment**: This mechanism explains a _visual_ effect the model needs (dark holes even where gas might still exist) via a cause the model doesn't represent (radiation field / ionization state) — a case of **"captures the look but for a different, simpler reason."** The model's dark cavities are dark because density is literally deleted by the negative splat, which is a reasonable phenomenological substitute for "this gas got ionized and had its PAHs destroyed" _for young HII-knot bubbles specifically_ (where real destruction is concentrated per Egorov). But it's a poorer match for the older dust "bubble" population, where the real driver of the observed dark cavity is sweeping/dilution of column density (a genuine density deficit, matching the negative-splat model well) rather than in-situ PAH photodestruction. No new machinery is needed for the visual outcome — the negative-splat mechanism is directionally correct for both populations even though the physical causes differ (density evacuation vs. molecular destruction) — but if the model ever wants a physically-motivated _brightness gradient_ at the cavity edge (a sharp destruction front right at the ionization boundary vs. a gradual density taper), that would need the HII-knot splats specifically to carry a steeper edge profile than the older dust-bubble splats, since Egorov's relation is steep and threshold-like (tied to ionization parameter) rather than the smooth radial falloff a pure density model would naturally produce.
